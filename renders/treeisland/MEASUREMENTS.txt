Tree of Life - Floating Island: the import report, the scene statistics, and what it costs
==========================================================================================

Brief §28 and §31. Every number here was measured on this branch on 2026-09-18 with
build/release. The pixels are not committed (the repository's rule for render evidence);
stills.json and preview.json beside this file regenerate all of them:

    ./build/release/src/avgen --headless --queue renders/treeisland/stills.json
    ./build/release/src/avgen --headless --queue renders/treeisland/preview.json

The design rationale is docs/decisions/ADR-337. The asset provenance is
assets/treeisle.manifest.json.


1. WHAT WAS IMPORTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

asset                        triangles  meshes  materials  textures  file      bounding size (x,y,z)
---------------------------  ---------  ------  ---------  --------  --------  ---------------------
tree-of-life-hero            3,162,186       7          7         0  139.6 MB  116.28 x 77.62 x 93.80
tree-of-life-hero-glowmere   3,162,186       7          7         0  139.6 MB  116.28 x 77.62 x 93.80
floating-isle                   64,171       9          9        28   42.1 MB    1.44 x  1.86 x  1.66
floating-isle-bare              32,709       2          2         6   12.3 MB    1.00 x  0.84 x  1.12

The scene loads `tree-of-life-hero-glowmere` and `floating-isle-bare`: 3,194,895 triangles,
nine glTF entities, nine materials, six textures, plus one procedural node of ~1,700 star points.
The engine's flatten reports `4 node(s) -> 9 entities, 9 meshes` for the glTF half and
`1 procedurals` for the stars.

The tree is **wider than it is tall** -- 116.3 across against 77.6 high, a canopy spreading 1.5x
the tree's height. That is what set the island scale, not the other way round.

What was deliberately left out. `FloatingIsleSubstance.spp` is a 214 MB Substance Painter source
file, not an asset. `Clouds.mat` has no mesh assigned and no textures of its own. The four
`.blend` files are the tree's source, and the hero GLB is the export the pipeline made from them.
The 24 reference renders in `hero_pass/renders/` are reference, per §2; `Glowmere.png` is what the
material palette was read off and nothing was matched to it pixel for pixel.


2. THE TWO UNITY CONVERSIONS THAT ARE EASY TO GET WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The island's nine `.mat` files are Unity URP Lit. Measured channel content of the mask maps:

material   R (metallic)        G (occlusion)       A (smoothness)
---------  ------------------  ------------------  ------------------
Chimney    0.000 (flat)        mean 0.334          0.337 (flat)
Decor      mean 0.149          1.000 (flat, none)  mean 0.125
Fence      0.000               1.000               mean 0.005
Grass      0.000               mean 0.144          mean 0.091
House      mean 0.004          1.000               mean 0.110
Roof       0.000               1.000               mean 0.289
Stairs     0.000               1.000               mean 0.000
Stone      0.000               1.000               mean 0.247
Windmill   0.000               mean 0.448          0.000 (flat)

  * The B channel is zero in all nine: Unity's detail mask is unused here.
  * Only Chimney, Grass and Windmill carry real occlusion; the other six ship a flat white G.
  * Repacked to glTF ORM as R = 1 + s(G - 1), G = 1 - 0.5 * A, B = R_unity, with s the material's
    `_OcclusionStrength` (0.755 on Grass, 1.0 elsewhere) and 0.5 the `_Smoothness` scalar every
    one of the nine carries. Omitting that 0.5 ships the whole island half as rough as Unity
    renders it, which reads as "slightly too shiny" and as nothing else.
  * House is the only emissive material: `_EmissionColor` (2.268, 4.867, 0.586), an HDR tint,
    exported as emissiveFactor (0.466, 1.0, 0.120) with KHR_materials_emissive_strength 4.867.
    It only appears in the cottage variant.


3. SCALE AND PLACEMENT, AND HOW THEY WERE CHOSEN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Measured off the geometry rather than guessed:

  tree, glTF Y-up      x [-60.02, 56.27]  y [-2.13, 75.49]  z [-51.26, 42.54]
  tree ROOT footprint  x [-20.21, 17.72]  y [-2.13,  5.00]  z [-14.87, 18.86]   <- radius ~20
  bare island          x [ -0.50,  0.51]  y [-0.73,  0.11]  z [ -0.74,  0.38]
  grass plateau top    y ~ 0.030 to 0.052, flat out to radius ~0.52 from its centre

The wide x/z extents of the tree's wood mesh at y = 20..40 are its *boughs*, not its roots. Taking
that 38-unit figure for the root spread would have demanded an island 1.8x larger than the one
this scene uses, and the tree would have sat on a plate.

  island scale       56x        (recorded here so it can be moved; it is one number in the scene)
  island position    [0, 0, 9.16]   recentres the rock's bounding box on the rotation axis
  tree position      [2.0, 3.2, 1.0]  trunk axis over the plateau centre, roots ~1 unit under grass
  tree scale         1.0        (native; the island moves, not the hero)

Which gives, in world units: plateau radius ~29, island 56 wide and 47 deep below the surface,
canopy 116 wide overhanging the island by ~30 a side, crown at y = 78.7 and the island's lowest
rock at y = -38.4. A 117-unit subject.

  The cottage arm settles §8 against §34. At this island scale the house is 57 world units tall

  §13 asks for.

  The as-exported arm settles §15. Same light, same island, same camera; a dark green tree with no
  bioluminescence of any kind. 5.1% of pixels above black against the Glowmere variant's 12.9%.


5. PERFORMANCE BASELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

tools/render_bench.py, 1920x1080, tier realtime, 180 frames per run with the first 12 dropped as
warm-up, 3 interleaved repeats per arm. One of six runs started while a build was running and is
excluded; the remaining five are uncontended and the per-run medians spread 1-2%.

preset                         runs   wall     p10     p90   spread     gpu   scene
-----------------------------  ----  -----   -----   -----   ------   -----   -----
tree-of-life-floating-island      2  57.94   53.25   63.23       1%   15.96   13.04
_compare-hero-materials           3  57.98   53.25   63.21       2%   15.79   12.85

GPU pass medians for the deliverable, in milliseconds:

  scene 12.78   depth 1.25   ao 0.39   shadow 0.26   post/bloom 0.20   tonemap 0.13
  post/composite 0.07   post/fxaa 0.07   clusters 0.00   background 0.00   shadowmask 0.00

  sum of pass medians / GPU frame median = 0.97. The frame is the passes; nothing is hiding
  outside them, which on a shared GPU is the check that says the number is the scene's and not
  somebody else's.

  CPU p50 38.67 ms, p90 43.86, p99 46.69. `wall` is higher than both because it includes readback
  and PNG/H.264 encoding; the 60-second preview encoded 1800 frames at 1280x720 in 81.5 s (22.1
  frames per second end to end).

  GPU errors: 0 on every run.

Two things this table says.

  3.16 M triangles is not what costs here. Nine entities, nine draws, no skinning, no instancing,
  no transparency: it is a draw-call-cheap way to spend a triangle budget, and 15.96 ms at 1080p
  leaves room for the particles, atmospherics and audio reactivity §28 says are coming.

  The Glowmere material variant is free. 15.96 against 15.79 ms is inside the 1-2% run spread, as
  it should be -- the two files differ only in seven material definitions.

`visible` and `tris` come back 0 from the harness for this scene. Those counters report the GPU
instance cull, and this scene's geometry is nine plain glTF entities that do not go through it.
They are *not reported*, not measured as zero.

The starfield's effect on shadowing was checked against a no-stars control, because the shell
pushes the scene radius from ~75 to ~1090 and the far plane with it (`max(radius * 50, 2000)`, so
54,500 rather than 3,750), and the shadow cascades are split across that range. It does not
degrade them: the plateau reads 53 sRGB with the stars and 47 without -- the difference is the
stars' own emission reaching the surface -- and the `shadow` pass costs 0.26 ms either way.


6. ANIMATION VALIDATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Brief §27. Two halves, one measured in pixels and one in transforms.

In pixels: renders/treeisland/rotation-stills/, five frames at t = 0, 37.5, 75, 112.5 and 150 s,
five distinct hashes, five clearly different aspects of the same tree. The trunk stays planted at
the same point on the plateau in every one; the grass patch and the rock silhouette turn with it.
And renders/treeisland/rotation-preview-60s.mp4, 1800 frames of continuous motion -- 120 degrees
of the revolution -- at 30 fps.

In transforms: tests/unit/test_treeisland_example.cpp asserts
`nodeWorldTransform(tree) == nodeWorldTransform(island) * treeLocal` to 1e-4 across four island
poses, with `treeLocal` checked unchanged at each, and with a control that the island's world
transform actually moved between samples (a band, 3 to 30 -- every comparison would pass with the
hierarchy deleted if the island stood still). Reparenting the tree off the island makes it fail at
three of the four samples; the fourth is the identity pose and correctly still passes.

There is no drift to measure because there is no state to drift: `LfoSource::update` is
`renderTime * rate + phase`, evaluated fresh every frame.
