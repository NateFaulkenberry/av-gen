# Strange Attractor — Art Pass 2
## Extended Dimensional Journey, Unexplained Encounters & UFO Sequence

### Project objective

Extend the existing Strange Attractor scene into a much longer, interconnected journey through multiple environments.

The first art pass established:
- The original sandstone canyon and river.
- The Matrix geometry scanning and data-corruption effects.
- A dimensional portal embedded in the canyon.
- A lunar environment with a crater portal.
- A return to the canyon at dusk.

The next art pass should extend this sequence substantially, introducing several new environments, unexplained encounters, and increasingly surreal events.

The camera should continue to behave as an invisible observer traveling through these environments. It is discovering things that appear to exist independently of its presence.

**Do not turn this into a conventional science-fiction narrative.** Avoid obvious storytelling, explanatory graphics, cinematic title cards, or overly literal visual exposition. The appeal is in discovering strange things happening in otherwise convincing environments.

Preserve the existing canyon and the established Matrix/portal visual language.

The camera journey should now follow this sequence:

1. Moon — transition from sunlight into the dark side.
2. Moon — geometric crater domes illuminating the dark landscape.
3. Moon — return through the crater portal.
4. Canyon at dusk — first Sasquatch encounter.
5. Canyon — another portal.
6. Mars — ancient Cydonia pyramid landscape.
7. Mars — portal embedded in one of the pyramids.
8. Canyon at night — second Sasquatch encounter, now accompanied by an alien and UFO.
9. Canyon — underwater portal.
10. Ocean — underwater continental shelf and alien submarine base.
11. Underwater base — portal inside the launch bay.
12. UFO interior — alien examination/observation scene.
13. UFO interior — a Gray alien detects the camera and projects a beam from its forehead.
14. Canyon at dawn — return through a violent data-moshing transition.
15. Final encounter — two Sasquatches levitating and manipulating a goat.
16. Camera flies past the encounter and lands at a suitable location to end the sequence.

This is an ambitious offline scene. Visual quality, environment construction, and convincing transitions take precedence over the live 1080p/30fps target.

# Phase 1 — Extend the lunar journey

## 1.1 Transition from the sunlight boundary into darkness

The first portal crossing already brings us to the Moon.

Refine and extend the lunar camera journey so that it appears as though we have emerged directly onto the boundary between the sunlit and dark portions of the lunar surface.

The camera should initially be surrounded by a strongly illuminated lunar landscape.

As it travels forward, we move toward the dark side of the Moon.

Visual progression:

- Begin in strong directional sunlight.
- Gradually approach the terminator, the boundary between lunar day and night.
- Allow the landscape to become progressively darker.
- Introduce increasingly long shadows.
- Eventually enter the dark portion of the lunar landscape.
- In the distance, reveal enormous geometric domes integrated into impact craters.

The transition from daylight into darkness should be spatially convincing.

Do not simply fade the lighting down across the entire environment. The camera should physically travel across the boundary between illuminated and unilluminated terrain.

## 1.2 Geometric crater domes

This is a new major environmental feature.

In the distance, we should see several enormous geometric structures integrated into lunar craters.

Think of a civilization that has constructed large illuminated domes within existing impact craters.

Visual direction:

- Large hemispherical or slightly faceted domes.
- Integrated into crater walls and crater floors.
- Geometric surface patterns.
- Warm internal illumination, potentially amber, gold, or pale white.
- Light spilling onto surrounding lunar terrain.
- Visible structural divisions and repeating architectural elements.
- Some domes partially obscured by crater rims and terrain.

The dark side of the Moon should be illuminated primarily by these structures.

The camera should travel toward the dark landscape with these illuminated structures visible in the distance.

Avoid making them look like conventional futuristic cities. They should feel ancient, mysterious, and monumental.

The crater structures should be recognizable from a considerable distance.

## 1.3 Return through the lunar crater portal

Retain the existing crater portal.

The camera should eventually approach it and pass through it, returning to the original canyon at dusk.

# Phase 2 — First Sasquatch encounter: Canyon at dusk

The camera emerges from the lunar portal and continues its existing trajectory toward the river.

In the distance, reveal three Sasquatch figures standing around a lit campfire.

## 2.1 Sasquatch models

Create three distinct Sasquatch figures.

Visual direction:
- Large, humanoid, ape-like silhouettes.
- Covered in dark brown or nearly black fur.
- Believable proportions and weight.
- Distinct individual silhouettes.
- Natural standing poses.
- Subtle idle movement.
- No exaggerated cartoon expressions.

They should be recognizable as Sasquatches without looking like conventional movie monsters.

Use suitable existing assets or source/create appropriate models.

The three figures should be positioned around a small campfire near the river or on a suitable section of the canyon floor.

## 2.2 Campfire

The campfire should be a genuine focal point in the distance.

Include:
- Visible flame animation.
- Flickering warm illumination.
- Light interacting with the nearby figures.
- Subtle smoke and embers.
- Reflections on nearby ground where appropriate.

The figures should be illuminated partially by the fire, allowing their silhouettes and fur to catch the light.

## 2.3 Camera choreography

The camera should notice the encounter but not approach too closely.

The viewer should have enough time to recognize the three figures and understand that they are gathered around a fire.

Continue the river-following camera trajectory toward another portal in the canyon walls.

Do not stop the entire sequence to frame the figures like a staged character introduction.

The camera is simply passing by something strange.

# Phase 3 — Mars: Cydonia Valley

The next canyon portal transports us to the surface of Mars.

The destination is an ancient, partially buried landscape inspired by the Cydonia region of Mars and the visual arrangement of the Giza Plateau in Egypt.

The environment should feature at least three large pyramidal structures.

## 3.1 Environment construction

Create a convincing Martian landscape.

Visual characteristics:
- Rust-red and ochre terrain.
- Large dusty plains.
- Rocky formations.
- Wind-shaped geological features.
- Subtle atmospheric haze.
- A muted, reddish sky.
- Distant terrain formations providing a sense of scale.

The landscape should feel ancient and abandoned.

Avoid a generic red desert with a few pyramids dropped into it.

The terrain should have enough visual complexity to support an extended camera journey.

## 3.2 The three pyramids

Build at least three large pyramidal structures arranged in a composition reminiscent of the Giza Plateau.

These should appear to be extremely old.

Important details:
- Weathered stone-like surfaces.
- Partially collapsed sections.
- Broken or eroded corners.
- Sand accumulated against the bases.
- Partially buried lower structures.
- Uneven surfaces and geological wear.
- Large enough to dominate the surrounding landscape.

They should look like ancient monuments that have been exposed to the Martian environment for an enormous period of time.

Avoid pristine, mathematically perfect Egyptian pyramids.

The structures should feel like remnants of an ancient civilization.

Consider giving each pyramid a slightly different state of preservation.

## 3.3 Mars camera journey

After passing through the canyon portal:

- Establish the Martian landscape.
- Reveal the three pyramids in the distance.
- Travel toward the pyramid group.
- Allow the scale of the structures to become increasingly apparent.
- Select one of the pyramids as the destination.
- Open a dimensional portal on or within that pyramid.

## 3.4 Pyramid portal

The portal should appear embedded in one of the ancient pyramids.

Explore an opening that appears to fracture or expose the underlying structure.

The surrounding stone may separate, disintegrate, or reveal the familiar computational effect.

The Matrix effect should connect the portal to the established Strange Attractor visual language.

The camera approaches and passes through the opening.

The destination is the original canyon.

We return to the canyon.

This time it is nighttime.

Instead:
- Glowing orange and red embers.
- Occasional small flickers.
- Thin wisps of smoke.
- A subtle residual light on the surrounding figures.

## 4.2 Three Sasquatches and one alien

However, a fourth figure has joined them.

This figure is a stereotypical Gray alien.

Visual characteristics:
- Slender humanoid body.
- Oversized smooth head.
- Large dark almond-shaped eyes.
- Small mouth and nose.
- Gray skin.
- Thin limbs.

Avoid cartoonish proportions or exaggerated expressions.

The alien and Sasquatches appear to be convening over something.

Do not make their interaction overly theatrical.

They should appear to be engaged in some kind of unexplained gathering or discussion.

Possible subtle behaviors:
- Standing in a loose circle.
- Looking toward a common point on the ground.
- Small head movements.
- Occasional gestures.
- Slight changes in posture.

The mystery is intentional.

## 4.3 UFO

Introduce a UFO parked on the ground somewhere near the gathering.

Visual direction:
- A substantial physical craft.
- Disc-like or otherwise recognizably extraterrestrial geometry.
- Subtle surface details.
- Dim lighting.
- Some illuminated windows or underside lighting.
- Possibly a small amount of atmospheric disturbance or residual landing effects.

The UFO should look physically present and integrated into the canyon environment.

It should not dominate the entire composition.

It is something the camera discovers nearby.

## 4.4 Camera choreography

The camera approaches the general area of the gathering.

Allow the viewer to register:
- The three Sasquatches.
- The additional Gray alien.
- The smouldering fire.

This should be a major environmental contrast to the previous terrestrial environments.

## 5.1 Underwater landscape

Create an expansive underwater environment.

The camera should emerge into a deep ocean landscape with a large continental shelf visible to one side.

Visual direction:
- Deep blue and blue-green water.
- Significant depth.
- Large underwater rock formations.
- A substantial continental shelf or underwater cliff.
- Subtle atmospheric particulate.
- Volumetric light shafts where appropriate.
- Natural underwater lighting.
- Distant terrain disappearing into the water.

The environment should feel enormous.

Avoid a brightly colored tropical reef aesthetic.

Think deep ocean exploration rather than shallow tropical water.

## 5.2 Underwater base

In the distance, reveal an enormous underwater installation built into or adjacent to the continental shelf.

It should resemble an extraterrestrial or otherwise unexplained underwater base.

Visual characteristics:
- Large structures integrated into the underwater rock formations.
- Illuminated architectural features.
- A prominent launch bay.
- Large openings in the structure.
- Visible interior lighting.
- Industrial or advanced construction.
- Substantial scale relative to the surrounding terrain.

The installation should look like it has been operating underwater for a very long time.

## 5.3 Submersible UFO-style vessels

Introduce small and medium-sized flying/submersible craft moving in and out of the launch bay.

- Craft departing the installation.

# Phase 6 — UFO interior: Mothership Zeta

This is a particularly important sequence.

The reference is the interior aesthetic and atmosphere of the Mothership Zeta DLC from Fallout 3.

Use that as a reference for the feeling of an alien spacecraft interior, not as a request to reproduce its exact architecture or assets.

The environment should feel like a genuine alien spacecraft with an unsettling, clinical, technologically advanced interior.

## 6.1 Interior architecture

Build a convincing interior space.

Possible characteristics:
- Curved or angular metallic architecture.
- Unusual structural geometry.
- Advanced technological surfaces.
- Integrated lighting.
- Metallic flooring.
- Large observation or examination areas.
- Strange machinery.
- Subtle environmental animation.
- A sense of substantial internal volume.

Avoid a generic corridor or a small empty room.

The camera should emerge into a room large enough to accommodate several humanoid figures and a central examination table.

## 6.2 Inhabitants

There are two groups of extraterrestrial humanoids inside the craft.

**Group one: Nordic/angelic humanoids**

Visual direction:
- Tall humanoid figures.
- Attractive, symmetrical facial features.
- Light-colored hair.
- Pale or lightly illuminated skin.
- Elegant proportions.
- Calm, controlled posture.
- Subtle clothing or garments.

They should have an unusual, almost angelic appearance.

Avoid stereotypical fantasy angel wings or medieval religious clothing.

Their appearance should be more like advanced humanoid beings than conventional fantasy characters.

**Group two: Gray aliens**

Use the established Gray alien visual language:
- Oversized heads.
- Large dark eyes.
- Gray skin.
- Slender bodies.
- Thin limbs.

Several may be present, but do not overcrowd the room.

The contrast between the tall, angelic humanoids and the Grays should be immediately recognizable.

## 6.3 Human examination subject

In the middle of the room, place a human man wearing a conventional suit.

He is strapped to a medical or examination gurney.

Visual characteristics:
- Dark business suit.
- White shirt.
- Conventional tie.
- Human appearance.
- Lying on his back.
- Restrained by straps across the body.

He should look completely out of place in the alien environment.

The surrounding aliens appear to be examining or attending to him.

Do not introduce graphic violence or gore.

The scene should be mysterious and unsettling rather than explicitly horrific.

## 6.4 Camera presence: Invisible observer

This is one of the most important technical and artistic details.

The camera has teleported into the UFO, but our presence is not physical.

We are effectively an invisible observer levitating in a corner of the room.

We should introduce a camera treatment that communicates that we have arrived through an unusual dimensional transition.

Explore:
- Subtle chromatic aberration.
- Temporal image displacement.
- Slight residual image distortion.
- Very subtle spatial warping.
- A faint digital reconstruction effect.
- Slight changes in image stability.

The effect should be restrained once the camera has arrived.

We are not supposed to be a physical object inside the room.

We are observing from an elevated corner position.

The camera should remain relatively still for a short period, allowing the viewer to understand the scene.

## 6.5 The Gray notices us

At some point, one of the Gray aliens notices the camera.

This is a critical story beat.

The alien should:
- Stop what it is doing.
- Slowly turn its head toward the camera.
- Establish direct eye contact.
- Hold that gaze briefly.

Then a purple beam of light projects from a triangular structure embedded in its forehead.

### Forehead triangle

The alien has a purple triangular structure in its forehead.

Visual characteristics:
- Clearly defined triangular shape.
- Glowing purple interior.
- Bright golden edges.
- Subtle internal illumination.
- A slightly unusual, non-organic appearance.

It should look like an integrated object rather than a painted symbol.

The beam emerges from this triangle and travels directly toward the camera.

Use a visually striking purple light beam.

The gold edges of the triangle should catch the light and remain visible.

The beam should appear to interact with our camera viewpoint.

## 6.6 Forced return / data-moshing escape

As the beam reaches the camera, the image begins to break apart.

The camera is forcibly displaced back into the canyon.

Use the established AV Gen data-moshing and Matrix effects.

This should be one of the most visually intense transitions in the entire sequence.

Explore:
- Strong temporal displacement.
- Frame tearing.
- Fragmented image reconstruction.
- RGB separation.
- Spatial warping.
- Digital fragment trails.
- Brief image instability.
- Geometry reconstruction.

The transition should feel like the Gray has detected our invisible presence and forcibly ejected us from the spacecraft.

Do not use a simple fade or dissolve.

The data-moshing should be the transition mechanism.

As the camera flies over the canyon and river, reveal another strange event.

## 8.2 Energy beams

Both Sasquatches project beams from their forehead triangles toward the goat.

Visual characteristics:
- Purple energy beams.
- Golden illumination around the triangle edges.
- Clearly visible beam geometry.
- Atmospheric interaction.
- Subtle illumination on nearby terrain.
- Beam convergence around the levitating goat.

The beams should look controlled and intentional.

They should visually match the Gray alien's forehead beam from the UFO scene.

This repetition establishes a visual relationship between the encounters without explaining it.

## 8.3 Levitating goat

A goat is suspended in the air between the two Sasquatches.

The goat should be recognizable as a real animal.

Use a convincing goat model with appropriate proportions and anatomy.

The goat should:
- Be suspended above the ground.
- Rotate slowly around its own axis.
- Emit a subtle glow.
- Occasionally distort or fragment.
- Temporarily disappear and reconstruct.
- Exhibit localized data-moshing effects.

The goat should look like it is being manipulated or held in some kind of energy field.

The beams should visibly connect the two Sasquatches to the goat.

The goat's appearance should combine physical realism with computational distortion.

Do not make it look like a cartoon or a generic glowing fantasy animal.

The visual should be strange, unsettling, and memorable.

## 8.4 Camera choreography

The camera flies past the encounter at a distance.

Do not approach too closely.

Give the viewer enough time to recognize:
- The two Sasquatches.
- Their glowing forehead triangles.
- The purple beams.
- The levitating goat.
- The goat's unusual rotation and digital distortion.

The camera should continue flying over the canyon and river.

Eventually find a suitable location to descend or land.

End the sequence there.

Do not introduce another portal or additional narrative event at this point.

This is the current endpoint of the scene. Leave the sequence open for future expansion.

# Phase 10 — Technical and artistic implementation

Before implementation, audit the existing scene and renderer to identify the most appropriate architecture for this expanded sequence.

Important considerations:

### Multiple environments

We now have:
- Original canyon, daytime.
- Original canyon, dusk.
- Original canyon, nighttime.
- Original canyon, dawn.
- Lunar landscape.
- Cydonia/Mars landscape.
- Underwater landscape.
- UFO interior.

Reuse the canyon geometry across its different time-of-day states.

Do not create redundant canyon geometry for each return.

Use appropriate lighting and atmosphere configurations for each state.

### Portals

Maintain the existing portal implementation where possible.

Every portal should:
- Have a visible destination.
- Reveal actual destination geometry.
- Support convincing perspective.
- Allow the camera to pass through.
- Avoid obvious scene cuts.

### Character animation

Prioritize believable silhouettes, positioning, and subtle movement.

Not every character needs elaborate animation.

For this scene, well-composed static or subtly animated figures are preferable to poorly executed complex character animation.

### Lighting

Lighting is essential to the success of the new environments.

Pay particular attention to:
- Lunar sunlight and terminator.
- Illuminated geometric domes.
- Campfire and ember illumination.
- Martian atmospheric lighting.
- Underwater volumetric light.
- Alien spacecraft interior lighting.
- Purple forehead beams.
- Dawn canyon illumination.

### Camera and pacing

This is now a longer sequence.

Do not rush between environments.

Each environment should have sufficient screen time for the viewer to understand its composition and discover its major visual features.

The camera should remain in continuous motion wherever practical.

Avoid arbitrary camera cuts.

### Performance

This is an offline-oriented art pass.

Prioritize visual quality and allow more expensive techniques where they materially improve the result.

However:
- Avoid unnecessary duplication of geometry.
- Reuse shared assets.
- Keep memory consumption manageable.
- Avoid introducing entirely separate rendering systems without justification.
- Maintain reasonable scene organization.
