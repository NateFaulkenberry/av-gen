# AV Gen Art Direction: Digital Ocean — Replace the Toy Sailboat with Credible Modern Vessels

(Owner brief, received 2026-10-15, saved verbatim.)

## Objective

Replace the existing childish-looking sailboat with one or more attractive, convincingly designed boats appropriate for a polished anime-inspired ocean scene.

The existing boat reads as a children's illustration rather than a designed vessel. Do not fix this by merely adding more details to the same silhouette. Redesign its proportions, silhouette, construction, and materials from the ground up.

The desired result is **real-world boat design translated into a sophisticated illustrated art style**.

Potential vessels include:
- A sleek modern motor yacht.
- A low-profile performance speedboat.
- A contemporary sailing yacht, if a sailboat fits the composition.
- A distant cargo ship or large vessel as a subtle background element, if it improves the sense of scale.

Do not add every vessel type. Select one or two that make the scene more beautiful and visually interesting.

## 1. Start with real boat design references

Before modeling anything, research real vessels and select a specific design direction.

Look at photographs and manufacturer imagery of:
- Modern performance motorboats.
- Luxury motor yachts.
- Contemporary sailing yachts.
- Sleek day cruisers and express cruisers.

Study real hull profiles, bow shapes, cabin proportions, windshields, decks, railings, windows, and how the vessel meets the water.

Select a small number of reference images and use them to guide the actual design. Do not invent a generic boat from the abstract idea of what a boat looks like.

Do not copy a specific manufacturer's branding or logos. Use real-world design references for proportions and construction.

## 2. Preferred direction: a sophisticated modern motor yacht

The first candidate should be a **sleek, modern motor yacht or performance day cruiser**, rather than the existing sailboat.

Its silhouette should communicate the vessel's identity immediately:

- A long, hydrodynamically plausible hull.
- A clean, tapered bow.
- A believable waterline and hull depth.
- A carefully proportioned cabin or windscreen.
- A low, streamlined deck profile.
- A coherent stern and transom.
- A few purposeful windows and deck details.
- A restrained, elegant color scheme.

The cabin must feel integrated into the boat's design, not like a box placed on top of a floating wedge.

Use long, sweeping contours and controlled angular transitions. The vessel should look expensive, engineered, and intentional.

Avoid exaggerated cartoon proportions, oversized cabins, tiny hulls, huge circular windows, stubby bows, and bulbous toy-like forms.

## 3. Alternative: a genuinely fast speedboat

If a speedboat suits the composition better, build a modern performance craft with a recognizable low, fast silhouette.

Design characteristics:
- Long, narrow hull.
- Sharp or carefully curved bow.
- Low windshield.
- Tapered, integrated cockpit.
- Clean deck surfaces.
- Credible stern and propulsion arrangement.
- A small number of high-contrast accents.
- A wake that communicates movement and speed.

The boat should look as if it could actually travel quickly across the ocean. Do not create an implausible wedge, floating car, or toy racing boat.

If the boat is moving fast, let the water tell that story through a directional wake and restrained spray rather than exaggerated cartoon splashes.

## 4. If retaining a sailboat, redesign it completely

Do not reuse the existing sailboat silhouette as the starting point.

A credible modern sailing yacht needs:
- A long, narrow hull with believable freeboard.
- A properly integrated deck and cabin.
- A tall, slender mast.
- Correctly proportioned sails with clear, purposeful shapes.
- A coherent relationship between the mast, boom, rigging, and hull.
- Restrained railings and other small details where visible.

Avoid a triangular sail placed on a generic hull, a mast that looks like a pole, oversized brightly colored sails, and exaggerated cartoon geometry.

Sails should feel like designed fabric surfaces, not rigid geometric flags. Their shape should relate to the wind direction and the vessel's orientation.

A sailboat is optional. Do not keep it merely because it already exists.

## 5. Sophisticated anime styling — not children's cartoon styling

Preserve the illustrated anime direction of Digital Ocean, but raise the visual maturity.

Use:
- Clean, elegant silhouettes.
- Carefully designed proportions.
- Broad, intentional color regions.
- Restrained cel shading.
- Selective highlights along the hull and cabin.
- Subtle color variation on sun-facing surfaces.
- A small number of fine details that establish scale and craftsmanship.

Avoid:
- Thick black outlines around every component.
- Oversized cartoon windows.
- Bright primary colors used indiscriminately.
- Random stripes, decals, or decorative graphics.
- Excessive bevels and tiny geometric details.
- Glossy plastic toy materials.
- Overly cute proportions or exaggerated features.

The goal is closer to a sophisticated anime film background, a beautifully art-directed illustrated travel scene, or a high-end animated sequence than a children's picture book.

The vessel can be simplified, but its underlying design must remain convincing.

## 6. Materials and color

Use a restrained palette that integrates with the ocean.

Possible combinations:
- Warm ivory hull with deep navy glazing and subtle brass or warm-sand accents.
- Pearl-white hull with muted blue-violet shadows and cool cyan reflections.
- Deep navy hull with pale upper surfaces and one controlled accent color.
- Warm off-white sails with carefully placed blue-violet shadows for a sailing yacht.

Avoid default saturated red, yellow, and blue toy-like color combinations unless the broader composition specifically calls for them.

Windows should read as dark glazing with controlled reflections, not as black circles or large cartoon eyes.

The hull should have a clear light-facing surface, shadow-facing surface, and waterline. Use just enough shading to describe its shape without introducing realistic material noise that conflicts with the illustrated ocean.

## 7. Give the boat a credible relationship with the water

The vessel must appear to sit *in* the ocean, not float above it.

Implement, using existing AV Gen capabilities where possible:
- Correct waterline placement.
- A subtle hull shadow or contact-darkening effect if supported.
- A restrained bow wave.
- A trailing wake appropriate to the vessel's speed.
- Small, graphic foam accents that follow the water shader's visual language.
- Gentle vertical movement or rocking only if appropriate to the scale of the waves.

For a cruising yacht, use a modest wake and gentle movement.

For a fast speedboat, use a longer directional wake and more energetic, but still controlled, foam.

Do not surround the boat with a giant white cartoon splash. Do not use a generic circular foam ring. The wake should be directional and consistent with the boat's motion.

The vessel's movement and wake must agree. If the boat moves across the scene, the wake should trail behind it.

## 8. Scale and composition

The boat is a supporting scene element, not a new focal point that overwhelms the ocean.

- Place it at a believable scale relative to the island, dolphins, waves, and horizon.
- Keep it large enough to recognize its vessel type at live-performance resolution.
- Use perspective and placement to establish distance rather than simply scaling every boat to the same size.
- Avoid lining boats up evenly like decorative props.
- If using two vessels, vary their distance, orientation, and visual weight.
- Keep the primary wave forms and sunlight readable around them.

Prefer one excellent yacht or speedboat over several mediocre vessels.

## 9. Animation

Keep animation restrained and physically coherent.

For a cruising yacht:
- Slow translation across the scene.
- Subtle bobbing and rolling.
- A wake that follows its trajectory.
- Small changes in sunlight and reflections.

For a speedboat:
- Faster directional movement.
- A longer wake that responds to speed.
- Restrained hull pitch if the wave conditions warrant it.
- A small amount of spray or foam.

For a sailing yacht:
- Sail orientation should relate to wind direction.
- The hull should move consistently with the sails and water.
- Avoid exaggerated flapping unless a visible gust justifies it.

Do not spin the vessel, oscillate it mechanically, or animate every component independently. The boat should feel like a coherent object moving through a coherent environment.

## 10. Required visual iteration

Produce three candidates using the same camera and scene:

**A — Modern luxury motor yacht**
A long, elegant white hull, integrated cabin, dark glazing, restrained accents, and a gentle wake.

**B — Performance speedboat**
A low, streamlined hull, sharp bow, integrated windshield, a more dynamic silhouette, and a directional wake.

**C — Contemporary sailing yacht**
A credible sailing vessel with a slender mast, well-proportioned sails, and an elegant hull.

Render each in the actual Digital Ocean scene. Evaluate the silhouette first, then proportions, materials, and water interaction.

Choose the candidate that contributes most to the scene. If none is good enough, revise the design before adding more detail.

## Implementation constraints

- Inspect and replace the existing boat asset rather than layering new details onto a fundamentally poor design.
- Reuse the current AV Gen scene, material, animation, and rendering infrastructure where practical.
- Do not introduce a general-purpose boat simulator or a new physics system.
- Keep geometry and animation efficient for real-time performance.
- Ensure the boat reads clearly at the intended live render resolution.
- Capture before-and-after screenshots and include the chosen reference direction in the final report.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The vessel has a credible, recognizable real-world boat silhouette.
- [ ] Hull, bow, stern, deck, cabin, and windows have convincing proportions.
- [ ] The design reads as sophisticated illustration rather than a children's cartoon.
- [ ] Materials and colors fit the anime-inspired ocean.
- [ ] The boat sits naturally on the water with a coherent wake.
- [ ] Animation is restrained and consistent with the chosen vessel type.
- [ ] The boat is recognizable at live-performance resolution.
- [ ] The agent has compared multiple silhouettes and selected the strongest.
- [ ] The old toy-like boat has been removed rather than cosmetically patched.

**Final direction:** Design a real boat first, simplify it second, and stylize it third. Never use stylization as an excuse for bad proportions.
