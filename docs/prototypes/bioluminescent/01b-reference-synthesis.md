# Bioluminescent ecosystem: reference synthesis

45 images: 12 terrestrial (A), 12 marine (B), 7 macro/network (C), 7 night atmosphere (D), 7 concept/film (E). Bracketed numbers refer to files in `refs/`.

## The colour gamut nature actually uses

Real bioluminescence uses a narrow band of hues, each tied to a habitat:

- **Marine blue, 470-490 nm.** Seawater transmits blue best. Dinoflagellate flashes peak at about 472-475 nm [13-15]; ctenophores at about 486 nm. Haddock, Moline & Case 2010, https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-marine-120308-081028; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9656108/
- **Glowworm cyan-blue, 487 nm** [10, 11]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arachnocampa_luminosa
- **Millipede cyan-green, 495 nm** [12]. Marek et al. 2011, https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822(11)00887-6
- **Fungal green, 520-530 nm,** continuous, on a circadian rhythm [01-07]. Desjardin et al., https://repository.yu.edu/bitstreams/bf885333-a1f7-4f65-a169-4cd8c53254a3/download
- **Firefly yellow-green, about 560 nm** (*Photinus pyralis*), visibly warmer than fungal green [08, 09]. https://pubs.acs.org/acsodf/article/3/3/2628/749659/Firefly-Luciferase-Mutant-with-Enhanced-Activity
- **Red is rare.** The railroad worm's head lantern emits at 623 nm, the only red beetle luciferase (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-44534-3). The dragonfish *Malacosteus* emits far-red at about 705 nm (https://doi.org/10.1126/science.225.4661.512).

**Often mistaken for bioluminescence:** ctenophore rainbows are diffraction off comb plates [18, 19] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ctenophora); coral "neon" is fluorescence under actinic blue [24]; the red siphonophore dots [21] are pigment lit by ROV lamps.

Violet, magenta and pink are essentially absent from real emission. The palette runs cyan through green to yellow, with one red accent.

## Brightness and darkness

Darkness fills 60-95% of every real frame [01, 02, 07, 10, 12, 13, 20]. Emitters are so faint that cameras need long exposures; [05] barely clears the noise floor. Any conventional light swamps them: moon and town lights [13, 16], a torch [10], a flashlight [38].

Fungal emitters stay saturated at their brightest and never clip to white [01-04]. Near-white cores appear only in fast, intense marine flashes [22] and the densest overlaps.

## Clustering

- **Size-graded clusters:** a few large fruiting bodies trailing into many millimetre specks [02].
- **Lines along substrate features:** wave crests [13, 14], ceiling cracks [10], wood grain [06], sand drainage lines [16].
- **Regular rows:** photophores [23], comb rows [19, 20], siphonophore modules [21].
- **Points scattered through a volume,** densest in clearings [08, 09].

## Do small sources light their surroundings?

Almost never. Fungi leave the log beneath them black [01-03], glowworms leave the limestone dark [10], fireflies light no leaves [08]. The only spill is a faint reflection on wet ground [04, 12] and a centimetre rim where an emitter touches its host [03]. Collectively, at distance, they form a texture of points that defocus or fog turns into soft haze [03, 09], not illumination.

## How film departs from this

Avatar [44] inverts the real photos: emitters cover most of the frame, cast fill light on characters, a cyan ambient removes black, and violet, which nature barely uses, is a main hue. It gains readability and spectacle. Worth keeping: rim-lit translucent discs, a hierarchy of emitter scales, a strict palette.

Annihilation [45] gets its strangeness from the air, not from glowing objects: the scene stays photographic while faint spectral fringes drift through the haze. Haeckel [40-43] and Rousseau [39] supply form and silhouette, not light.

## Rules for the environment

1. **Darkness is the default.** At least 70% of the frame within 1-2 stops of black.
2. **Emitters do not light the world.** No shadow-casting lights for fungi, plankton or insects; only a centimetre contact rim and reflections in wet surfaces or water [12, 36].
3. **Palette: cyan to yellow-green.** Fungi and plants 520-530 nm, water and air life 470-490 nm, insects about 560 nm, one rare red accent. Violet and magenta only for non-emissive iridescence or fluorescence.
4. **Emitters stay saturated.** No tone-mapping to white except brief, intense flashes.
5. **Glow has internal structure:** gills, segments, veins or lattice, never a flat fill [04, 12, 25, 42].
6. **Place emitters along features** (edges, cracks, grain, crests, drainage), never as uniform scatter.
7. **Grade cluster sizes:** few large, many tiny. At distance collapse to points or haze, a physically honest LOD.
8. **Disturbance triggers marine and air life:** a flash on contact that decays in 0.1-3 s [13-15, 22]. Beats become disturbances. Fungi stay steady and only breathe slowly.
9. **Emission masks per organ:** stalk not cap, gills not top [05, 07]. Non-emitting parts give silhouette.
10. **Ambient is a very dim, cool moonlit blue** [35, 37]. Fog gets darker and bluer with distance [33, 34]; the sky stays the brightest large area.
11. **Iridescence is view-dependent and segmented** [19]: small travelling segments of spectral colour, never a tint.
12. **Strong conventional light kills the effect** [10, 38]. Keep player and camera lights off or below emitter level.
