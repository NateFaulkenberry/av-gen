# DIGITAL MOSH: Research

Brief §1. Two investigations, kept apart until the end. The first is how the Surrealist painters made the
impossible convincing. The second is what digital corruption actually is. The last section says what each
finding obliges the scene to do. Every rule in `02-design.md` traces back to a numbered principle here
(S1-S14 for the painters, G1-G12 for the glitch work, P1-P8 for perception).

Sources are listed at the end. The painting descriptions were checked against the works themselves (MoMA,
Wikipedia's catalogue entries, the artists' own statements), not against summaries of "surrealism".

---

## Part 1. The Surrealists

### Salvador Dalí

**The Persistence of Memory (1931).** It is a small painting (24 × 33 cm). It has four elements on a stage:
a dead olive tree growing out of a table-like block, three soft watches, a fleshy profile lying on the
ground, and the cliffs of Cap de Creus lit by a low sun. A shadow from outside the frame, which Dalí said was
Mount Puig Pení, covers the whole foreground. More than two-thirds of the canvas is brown, grey, ochre and
dark blue. There is no red or green. The objects sit far apart, so the picture is mostly empty beach, water
and sky.

What makes it work:

- **S1. The landscape is real, and documented.** Dalí painted a real coastline he knew intimately, with
  photographic finish. The impossible objects borrow credibility from it. Unlike Tanguy, Dalí anchors his
  dream in a geography. *The world must be the most physically convincing thing in the frame.*
- **S2. One rule is broken per object, and the rest of its physics is kept.** The watches are soft, but
  they hang under gravity, drape over edges, cast correct shadows and keep their numerals and gold cases.
  Softness is the only violation. A melting watch that also glowed, floated and rotated would read as
  fantasy, not as a dream. *Each corruption event breaks exactly one law.*
- **S3. The shadow comes from outside the frame.** The largest shape in the picture is a shadow whose cause
  the viewer cannot see. This is the strongest source of unease in it. *Shadows are a free, cheap, powerful
  instrument: a shadow with no visible caster, or the wrong caster.*
- **S4. Hand-painted dream photographs.** Dalí's phrase for his paintings. Their finish is smooth and
  polished, with no visible brushwork. The precision is the point: dreams are not blurry in the experience
  of having them. *Early frames must be clean: no grain, no haze noise, no glitch.*

**The Elephants (1948) and The Temptation of Saint Anthony (1946).** Animals stand on legs so long and thin
that they cannot carry the bodies. The legs are drawn with real joints, and the bodies are solid and
heavy. The horizon is very low, so the animals tower against an enormous sky. In *The Temptation*, Saint
Anthony is small in the bottom-left corner, holding up a cross. The procession comes at him along a
diagonal, and clouds cut the top of the picture.

- **S5. Scale is a measurement, and it is broken by measuring it.** The stilt legs work because the bodies
  are recognisably elephants of known weight. A recognisable object of known size placed against no scale
  reference becomes unmeasurable. *Keep at least one object of known scale (a tree, a stone, a doorway-sized
  thing), then make it the wrong size relative to its shadow, its reflection or its neighbour.*
- **S6. A low horizon and a vast sky make objects heroic and isolated.** About 70-80% of these canvases are
  sky or ground with nothing in it. *Compose with a horizon in the lower third or the upper third, never the
  middle.*

**Dream Caused by the Flight of a Bee Around a Pomegranate a Second Before Awakening (1944).** It shows a
causal chain that unfolds in an instant. A pomegranate bursts, a fish leaps out of it, two tigers leap out
of the fish, and a rifle with a bayonet follows them toward a sleeping woman. The woman floats above a flat
rock, which floats above a flat sea. A Bernini elephant on stilt legs walks along the horizon. The chain
runs on one diagonal from lower left to upper right, accelerating. Two drops of water hover beside the
pomegranate. Dalí said it was the first painting of Freud's discovery that a dream with a long narrative
can be caused by a single external stimulus in the instant before waking.

- **S7. Transformation is a chain of containment.** One thing bursts and the next thing comes out of it,
  each larger and more dangerous than the last. This is the brief's §4 chain (tree → bend → liquid →
  fragments → particles → pixels → another object) in Dalí's own grammar. *Corruption must be a chain in
  which each state visibly emerges from the previous one, never a cut between states.*
- **S8. Things hover a little above their ground.** The rock hovers above the sea, the woman hovers above
  the rock, and the drops hover in the air. Each gap is small, and each has a cast shadow that proves it.
  *A small gap between an object and its shadow is more uncanny than a large flight.*
- **S9. A dream's length is not its duration.** The whole narrative fits into the second before waking.
  *Temporal corruption should compress and stretch time locally (one object in slow motion, another
  frozen), not globally.*

### Yves Tanguy

In *Indefinite Divisibility* (1942), *Mama, Papa is Wounded!* (1927) and *The Furniture of Time* (1939), a
continuous grey-ochre ground fades into a grey-lavender sky with no horizon line. The two are separated
only by a change of value and haze. On this plane stand smooth, bone- or pebble-like forms with no
nameable identity. Each casts a precise, hard shadow, and some float with shadows beneath them. The light
is a flat, low, theatrical source.

- **S10. A world without a horizon line is infinite and placeless, not unfinished.** The ground dissolves
  into the sky through atmosphere. This answers the owner's dead-space rule directly. *The world never ends
  at an edge; it ends in haze. The ground and the sky meet in a value gradient, never in a line or a black
  void.*
- **S11. Biomorphic forms with exact shadows.** Tanguy's objects are not recognisable, but the light on
  them is completely consistent: their shading, contact shadows and cast shadows are precise. Their
  identity is ambiguous, and their physical presence is not. *Our stones are smooth, rounded, singular
  forms of indeterminate size, lit with complete consistency.*

### René Magritte

*The Empire of Light* (1953-54) shows a night street under a daylight sky. Nothing else in it is fantastic.
Its power comes from a single rule change inside a completely ordinary picture. *The Listening Room* (1952)
shows an ordinary green apple filling an ordinary room. Neither the apple nor the room is distorted; only
their relation is wrong. In *The Castle of the Pyrenees* (1959), a huge boulder with a castle on top hangs
above the sea, painted like a geology textbook illustration. *Not to Be Reproduced* (1937) shows a man
looking into a mirror and seeing the back of his own head.

- **S12. Substitution and relation, not deformation.** Magritte almost never deforms an object. He changes
  one relation: day/night, inside/outside, big/small, reflection/object. *The Uncanny stage is built only
  out of relation errors: a reflection that does not match, a shadow at the wrong angle, a light that
  belongs to another time of day, a cloud that does not move.*
- **S13. The impossible object is painted plainly.** The floating boulder has no glow and no motion lines.
  Gravity's failure is shown as calmly as the sea. *Impossible states get the same lighting and materials as
  possible ones.*

### Max Ernst

Ernst used frottage (pencil rubbings of floorboards and leaves, 1925), grattage (scraping paint over
textures, 1927) and decalcomania (pressing wet paint between surfaces, taken from Domínguez in 1936).
*Europe After the Rain II* (1940-42) used decalcomania for a landscape that reads as rotted architecture,
coral, flesh and moss at once. His collage novels (*La femme 100 têtes*, *Une semaine de bonté*) graft
animal heads onto Victorian engravings so seamlessly that the joins vanish. In his own words, the guiding
principle is "a linking of two realities that by all appearances have nothing to link them, in a setting
that by all appearances does not fit them".

- **S14. Texture is a found process, and an image can be grown rather than drawn.** Ernst let a mechanical
  process make the texture, then read creatures into it. *Corruption should be generated by a process the
  scene runs (a spreading simulation), and the forms it makes should be read, not designed one by one.* The
  collage principle also gives the material-corruption rule: one object's surface grafted seamlessly onto
  another (stone wearing the tree's bark, the sky in a stone).

### Giorgio de Chirico

*Mystery and Melancholy of a Street* (1914) uses two contradictory vanishing points. The lit arcade's lines
converge slightly above the horizon, and the dark arcade's lines converge where a box van's roof meets the
yellow ground. A girl runs with a hoop toward a shadow whose caster (a statue?) is hidden behind a
building. The horizon is high, the light is late afternoon, and the shadows are enormously long. James
Thrall Soby said the geometry "has been deliberately modified for the purposes of poetic suggestion".
*The Melancholy of Departure* and *Gare Montparnasse* place a distant train and a clock showing a time that
does not match the light.

- **D1. Two perspectives in one frame.** Each half of the picture is internally consistent, and together
  they are irreconcilable. The viewer cannot settle on a ground plane. *Spatial corruption is two locally
  correct spaces stitched together: a region of the world rendered from a slightly different camera, or a
  horizon that steps.*
- **D2. Long shadows of absent things.** The statue is known only by its shadow. *The scene keeps a shadow
  on the ground whose caster is never seen (S3 again, independently).*
- **D3. Time is stopped but not frozen.** The flag still flies, and the train still steams. In the late
  stillness, one small motion becomes enormous. *In the Dream stage, the only motion is very small and very
  slow; a single moving thing is an event.*

### Surrealist theory

- Breton's first *Manifesto* (1924) defines Surrealism as "psychic automatism in its pure state ... the
  actual functioning of thought ... in the absence of any control exercised by reason". It adopts Pierre
  Reverdy's definition of the image (*Nord-Sud*, 1918): an image "cannot be born from a comparison but from
  a juxtaposition of two more or less distant realities. The more the relationship between the two
  juxtaposed realities is distant and true, the stronger the image will be." Breton added that the
  juxtaposition must arise from the unconscious, not be calculated.
- Lautréamont's "beautiful as the chance meeting on a dissecting table of a sewing machine and an umbrella"
  was the movement's emblem.
- Dalí's paranoiac-critical method (from 1930) organises unrelated pieces of reality around an obsessive
  idea, until the viewer sees double images (the *Swans Reflecting Elephants* type).

For a rendered piece, automatism means **the image is produced by a process the artist starts but does not
fully control**. Here that process is a simulation running on the music. The "distant realities"
principle means the corruption must bring together two things with no relation: a landscape painting and
a video codec. It must join them seamlessly, so that the joint is where the image happens.

## Part 2. Perception: why a violation is felt

- **P1. Prediction error.** Current models of perception (predictive processing; the work on the uncanny
  valley cited below) treat seeing as continuous prediction, with attention captured by prediction error.
  A violation is felt only against a confident prediction. *Hence the brief's contrast rule: the viewer
  must first be trained, by long stretches of lawful behaviour, to predict the world.*
- **P2. Repetition and the double.** Freud's 1919 essay "The Uncanny" lists the double, involuntary
  repetition, and uncertainty about whether something is alive or inanimate. Jentsch (1906) located the
  uncanny in intellectual uncertainty. *An object that appears twice, a cycle that repeats exactly,
  anything inanimate that turns toward the viewer.*
- **P3. Change blindness.** Slow changes across the whole field go unnoticed, and a large change made during
  a cut or a blink goes unnoticed too. Something is felt to be wrong before it is seen to be wrong. *The
  Uncanny stage uses changes slow enough to be missed and noticed only afterwards, such as the shadow
  angle drifting by 25° over 20 s.*
- **P4. Gaze is drawn to the face-like and the moving.** In an empty frame, one moving element takes all
  the attention. *Negative space amplifies everything placed in it (brief §14).*
- **P5. Loss of a ground plane is loss of balance.** The visual system takes "down" from the horizon and
  the ground's texture gradient. A tilting or stepping horizon is felt in the body. *Late-stage spatial
  corruption is horizon and ground-plane corruption, not camera shake.*
- **P6. Motion without cause.** Inanimate things moving with no visible force read as animate (the
  Heider-Simmel effect). *A stone that slides once, slowly, is more alarming than a stone that explodes.*
- **P7. Colour constancy.** We discount illuminant colour. A colour that spreads as light reads as
  atmosphere. The same colour spreading as surface pigment, against the light, reads as contamination.
  *The contagion must eventually stain surfaces, not only tint the light.*
- **P8. The return.** The ending works only if the recovered world is almost identical to the opening, so
  that the one remaining error is measured against a memorised baseline (P1 again).

## Part 3. Digital corruption: what it actually is

### How a codec breaks (datamoshing)

Inter-frame codecs (MPEG-4 Part 2, H.264) store a few complete I-frames. Every other frame is a P- or
B-frame: a field of **motion vectors per macroblock** (16×16, subdivided to 4×4 in H.264) plus a residual
correction. If an I-frame is removed, the decoder keeps applying the next shot's motion vectors to the
**old shot's pixels**.

- **G1. Moshing is motion with the wrong substance.** The image keeps moving correctly while its content
  belongs to the past. The result is not noise. It is coherent, fluid, organic motion, which is why
  Takeshi Murata's *Monster Movie* (2005) reads as liquid flesh. *A mosh is the present's motion carried
  by the past's colour.*
- **G2. Bloom.** If one P-frame is repeated, its motion is applied again and again, so moving regions smear
  and swell along their motion while still regions stay crisp. *Corruption concentrates where things move.
  A still world cannot be moshed, so stillness is protection.*
- **G3. Corruption is blocky because the codec thinks in blocks.** Macroblock edges, DCT ringing near
  edges, and 8×8 quantisation patterns are the "accent" of the medium (Menkman's *A Vernacular of File
  Formats*). *The grain of corruption must be an aligned grid. A world that rots into squares is rotting
  into its own representation.*
- **G4. Corruption propagates in time until the next keyframe.** An error persists and spreads frame after
  frame until an I-frame heals it. *Corruption has persistence (it does not flicker off on the next frame),
  and a healing event (a keyframe) resets it. In our language, a keyframe is the Recovery.*
- **G5. Errors smear in the scan direction.** A corrupted slice damages the rest of its row, then the
  following rows. *Corruption has a direction, horizontal-first.*

### Other forms

- **G6. Pixel sorting** (Kim Asendorf, *Mountain Tour*, 2010) sorts only the runs of pixels between two
  brightness thresholds. Regions inside the band dissolve into streaks, and the rest stays photographic.
  *Corruption that follows the image's own structure looks inevitable. Corruption laid on top of it looks
  like a filter.*
- **G7. Channel separation** is a property of real failures, such as an interlaced field offset or a
  convergence error in a CRT. It is the most clichéd glitch. *Use it only as a one-frame event on a
  transient, never as a constant.*
- **G8. Feedback** (video feedback, Steina and Woody Vasulka) makes self-similar, self-amplifying
  structures, which are the visual analogue of an infection. *Feedback is an amplifier: slow, persistent
  and unbounded unless a decay closes the loop.*
- **G9. Glitch is a failure of representation, not a style** (Menkman, *The Glitch Moment(um)*, 2011).
  Once glitch becomes a preset, it stops being a glitch: it is just a style. *Each corruption must appear
  as a failure of the specific thing being represented: a tree's corruption is about branching, a pool's
  about reflection, the ground's about tiling.*
- **G10. Representation strata.** A renderer builds the image from layers: world → objects → triangles or
  SDF → fragments → pixels → colour → light. A real failure exposes one of these layers. *The climax
  (brief §15) must expose them in order, each failure revealing the layer below: geometry → fragments →
  particles → temporal fragments → pixels (blocks) → colour (a palette collapse) → light (pure luminance).*
- **G11. Frozen frames and stutter** are what a decoder does when it is starved: it holds the last good
  frame. A world that stops while the sound continues is deeply wrong. *Temporal corruption uses held
  regions: part of the world freezes while the rest moves on.*
- **G12. Quantisation.** Posterisation and palette reduction lower the number of values. They give the
  look of a world losing its colour depth. *The Collapse uses it as the "colour" stratum.*

### Why corruption feels different from an ordinary VFX effect

An ordinary VFX effect has a **physical cause inside the fiction**: an explosion has a bomb, and fire has
fuel. Corruption has a cause **outside the fiction**, in the medium. It ignores the world's physics and
obeys the codec's: blocks, scan order, motion vectors, keyframes. The viewer reads it as damage to *the
picture*, not to *the place*. The brief's premise inverts this. **The place is the picture**, so codec
physics becomes the world's physics. Blocks appear in the terrain as terrain, and motion-vector smears
appear in the trees as trees. This is the synthesis.

## Part 4. Synthesis: the visual language

Calling it "Dalí plus RGB split" is the failure mode. The original idea that comes from combining the two
investigations:

> **The dream is a video that has lost its keyframe.** Its world was painted once, perfectly (the
> keyframe: Dalí's hand-painted dream photograph). Everything after is prediction, motion applied to the
> remembered image, and the predictions drift. Corruption is the world being *predicted* rather than
> *seen*: the brain's prediction error (P1) and the codec's prediction error (G1) are the same thing.

The rules this gives the scene:

1. **One law per event (S2, S12).** A corruption event breaks one law of one object and keeps every other
   law. The light, shadow and material stay correct while the shape goes soft. The shape stays correct
   while the shadow points the wrong way.
2. **Corruption is a substance in the world (G9, S14).** It is a contagion that lives on the ground,
   spreads by a process the scene simulates, and stains what it touches. It is not a filter over the
   frame. Image-space effects are allowed only where the world is already corrupted, and they are masked
   by it.
3. **The grain of the contagion is the block (G3).** As it advances, smooth organic surfaces gain an
   aligned square grid: quantised positions, stepped heights, square fragments. A world rotting into its
   own representation.
4. **Motion is what spreads it (G2).** The music's motion carries corruption. Still regions keep their
   integrity. A breakdown is literal recovery.
5. **A keyframe heals (G4, P8).** The Recovery is an I-frame: the world snaps back to the original
   painting, almost completely. Something survives the reset, the way a mosh survives a bad keyframe:
   one stone keeps its blocks.
6. **The world never ends (S10).** Ground fades into sky through haze and has no edge. Negative space is
   haze and plain, never black.
7. **Palette as temperature of the dream (S1, P7).** The colour goes from Dalí's ochre, cream and
   Cap-de-Creus blue, through Magritte's cold relation-errors (turquoise, violet), to the codec's
   primaries (magenta and chartreuse are the colours of a broken YUV→RGB conversion: a zeroed chroma
   channel decodes to green, and a saturated one to magenta). The corruption colour is not chosen for
   taste. It is the colour a broken decoder actually produces.

The last point is worth stating plainly. **Green and magenta are what failed video looks like.** When a
codec loses its chroma planes, the picture goes green, because Y with U = V = 0 decodes toward green. When
the chroma overflows, it goes magenta. The corruption palette is therefore the palette of the medium's own
failure, which makes the contagion colour meaningful rather than "neon".
