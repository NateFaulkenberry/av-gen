# Chorus Field: the Procedural Fiber Field spike

The brief is `00-brief.md`. The scene is generated by `examples/chorus-field/build.py`: edit the script and
run `python3 examples/chorus-field/build.py [look ...]`. Never edit the JSON it writes. The review stills,
contact sheets and clips are in `~/Desktop/av-gen-review/39-chorus-field/cp<N>-*/`.

The engine capability is two ADRs:
- [ADR-1180](../../decisions/ADR-1180-a-procedural-source-can-be-a-fiber.md): the Fiber source.
- [ADR-1181](../../decisions/ADR-1181-a-fiber-follows-the-streamlines-of-its-fields.md): the Streamline deformer
  and the strand pass.

## Phase 0: what the Echo Field is, and why it works

This is the production one, `examples/gpu-regression/echo-field.{json,scene.json}`, built on ADR-1116 and
ADR-1117.

| Aspect | What it is |
|---|---|
| Primitive | A 5-sided, 1-segment cylinder: 10 triangles, a dark stick. Nothing of the look comes from it. |
| Instance generation | The ADR-1117 "cells" kernel, a 2D hashed lattice on a 110 m disc, written per frame around the camera: 696k cells, 0.26 ms. |
| GPU buffers | 96 B `InstanceRecord`s (96 MB), base and live. The 0.4 MB audio ring rides in the field table (ADR-1116). |
| Vertex generation | The generic procedural vertex stage. It runs the deformer chain 4 times a vertex: 3 for a finite-difference normal and 1 for last frame's position. |
| Simulation | None. Every element is a pure function of (t, position, random), so seeks are exact. |
| Spatial calculation | The effector pass (0.98 ms): a spectrum field heard at a delay of distance / 9 m/s and banded by angle, plus onset fronts. |
| Culling | A deterministic prefix-scan frustum cull (0.26 ms) and indirect draws, inside the generator window. |
| Shading | Full PBR plus shadows. The emission is from the field. |
| Camera | A static, elevated three-quarter view. |
| Audio | Per element: its own band at its own moment, and the last 8 kicks as travelling fronts. |
| Performance (Ultra, 1080p) | **45 ms GPU**: scene pass 31 ms, shadows 10.6 ms, everything else ~1.5 ms. CPU is a flat 0.74 ms. |
| Useful counts | The handoff budgets ≤ ~250k visible thin elements for 60 fps. |
| Cheap | Generating, effectors, culling, and the CPU. |
| Surprisingly expensive | Drawing sub-pixel triangles with full PBR, shadows, and the 4× vertex chain. |

**Why it is compelling.** It is coherence: fields sampled at position make neighbours agree. The delay by
distance turns time into rings, and the angle-to-band mapping turns frequency into fans. The per-element random
only adds microvariation. **None of the look is in the primitive; all of it is the field.**

**What generalises:** records, generators, effectors, fields, the cull, and per-element audio.
**What stays scene-specific:** the disc, the spectrum rings and the bud colour.

**The smallest generalisation** is a primitive that lies *along* a field instead of sampling it once. That is
a fiber whose centre line is a streamline: ADR-1180 and ADR-1181, two additions to the existing procedural path.

## Research (what the decision rests on)

The question was: "what is the cheapest technically sound way to produce the phenomenon?"

- **Strand rendering in production:**
  - **TressFX / AMD and Frostbite hair:** strands are simulated or generated in compute into a vertex buffer,
    then expanded to camera-facing quads in the vertex shader, with a minimum width and coverage-based AA. ADR-1181's strand pass plus ADR-1180's strip is
    the same split, minus the simulation.
  - **Kajiya-Kay (1989) and Marschner (2003):** a thread is shaded by its tangent, not by a normal. A sub-pixel
    thread *must* be, or the highlight aliases into glitter. This was measured: CP1's glitter turned into CP2's
    coherent bands with no other change.
  - **Persson's "phone-wire AA":** clamp thin geometry to about a pixel and fade it by the coverage it lost.
    That is the pixel floor.
- **Flow visualisation:**
  - **Line-integral convolution (Cabral and Leedom 1993):** dense streamlines make a vector field visible as
    texture.
  - **Evenly spaced streamline seeding (Jobard and Lefer 1997):** structure reads when streamlines are long
    and seeded with intent. The Phase 1 failures are the measurement: short fibers filling a volume read as
    fur.
- **Fields:**
  - **Curl noise (Bridson 2007):** divergence-free turbulence. The engine already had it as a field kind, and
    the engine's vortex, spiral, attractor and onset fields cover the rest.
  - **Inward spirals** make rolls and coils.
  - **Counter-rotating eddy pairs** make wakes, which read as eye sockets.
- **Indirect draws and GPU culling:** already production (ADR-029). The fiber inherits them.
- **Professional audio-reactive systems** (Notch, TouchDesigner particle-force workflows): audio modulates
  *forces*, not the drawn elements. That is the Phase 5 mapping: audio routes weight individual streamline
  pulls, and onset fields push.

**Rejected after this research:**
- simulated advection with trails (state and seeking, for a picture that is a pure function of the field);
- curve control-point fits (they smooth away the tight turns that make wakes read);
- a separate renderer (the brief's §20).

## Phase 7: what is engine, and what is scene

The test applied to every piece: would a second scene (Storm Field, Void Field, Neural Field) need it unchanged?

**Engine (reusable, ADR-1180 and ADR-1181; reachable from scene JSON, parameters, the CLI and the editor):**

| Piece | Where | Why it is engine |
|---|---|---|
| Fiber source: a camera-facing strip, a pixel floor paid in coverage, a round-thread normal, a Kajiya-Kay normal when sub-pixel, LOD as fewer segments | `PrimitiveKind::Fiber`, `fiberVertex` | Every fiber scene needs thin metal that does not alias and does not glitter |
| Streamline deformer: steering, stiffness, tension, several summed pulls, midpoint integration | `DeformerKind::Streamline`, `fiberCentreLine` | It is the whole mechanism by which a field becomes visible structure |
| Strand pass: a centre line integrated once a record a frame, read by every pass | `fiber_strands.wgsl`, `procedural_renderer.cpp` | It turns O(segments²) per pass into O(segments) once; it is needed at any count |
| Per-pull routability: `procedural/<n>/deform/<k>/{amount,falloff,tension}` | the parameter registrar | Audio and MIDI weight one force without touching the others. This is how "the music changes topology" is authored |

**Already engine, and used as-is:**
- field kinds: vortex, spiral, attractor, direction, curl, radial vector, onset, spectrum and noise colour;
- compound fields (the kick shockwave is `radialVector × onset`);
- field transforms (a vortex scaled in depth is a tube);
- Scale effectors (a scalar field thins the fibers to zero);
- material programs with the `field` op (oxide against silver, and the socket-rim glow, per fragment);
- thin film (ADR-1143);
- routes with processor chains;
- Grid and generator distributions;
- GPU cull and indirect draws.

**Scene configuration (`examples/chorus-field/build.py`), and why it stays there:**
- **The entity:** the eye, temple, maw and chin fields and their positions. It is the art, and it is *one*
  configuration of the vocabulary.
- **The ceiling funnel, the curtain seeding and the debris layer:** composition.
- **`chorusWire`:** a material program. It is authored data.
- **The audio mapping:** routes in the project.
- **Lights, environment and cameras.**

**Not built, deliberately:**
- a "personality preset" system;
- a `ChorusField` node kind;
- a field mirror/symmetry flag (the eddy pairs are authored as pairs);
- a GPU 3D generator kernel (a Grid distribution was enough below 1M; a volumetric generator is the next step
  if an unbounded fiber world is wanted).

Each is a candidate once a second scene needs it, which is ADR-1117's "two use cases before a shared abstraction".

CPU work is flat at about 2 ms in every row. No frame reads the GPU back.

**What the numbers say:**
- **The look does not need many fibers.** The god is 5k long wires. Past about 20k visible, the image does not
  gain structure: the streams are already continuous, and more fibers only thicken them. It does pay in shadows
  (linear in fibers) and in the strand pass.
- **The material program is the largest single cost:** 8.4 ms at 1080p. It reads three fields per fragment
  (the face compound of four vortices, the eyes, and a gradient) over heavy overdraw. Shadows cost 2-4 ms, and
  segments cost ~0.2 ms per segment per 5k fibers across the strand pass and raster.
- **The scene pass barely scales with resolution** (19.7 ms at 1080p, 15.3 ms at 720p). Long one-pixel strips
  are raster-bound: thin triangles shade whole 2×2 quads at every edge.
- **The strand pass is not free with rich fields:** 2.5 ms for 5k × 24 segments with five pulls (two of them
  compounds) at two samples a step (RK2). Integrating only *visible* records (after the cull) would cut it in
  proportion, and is the first optimisation to make (below).

**The operating point:**
- **Offline (the stills and the clips):** 5k fibers × 48 segments at 1080p supersample 2. It is about 32 ms a
  frame, so rendering runs near real time.
- **Live at 60 Hz:**
  - 5k × 12 segments at a 720p canvas, program on, is 16 ms (≈60 fps).
  - Without the program and the shadows, 5k × 24 fits in about 12 ms.
- **120 Hz** is not reachable with the program as authored. It needs the program's field reads moved out of
  the fragment (next item) or the plain material.

**Next optimisations, in order of gain per effort:**
1. **Evaluate the program's field reads per vertex**, a program flag. The shear varies slowly along a wire, so
   fragment precision is wasted. This would recover most of the 8 ms.
2. **Integrate strands for visible records only:** dispatch over the cull's compacted list. This cuts the
   strand pass in proportion to the cull.
3. **Cast fiber shadows from a coarser LOD level** (fewer segments): shadows are linear in triangles.
