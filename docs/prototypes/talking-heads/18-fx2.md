# Talking Heads, fx2: four kinds of corrupted transmission

This stop answers the owner's signal-corruption brief (17-brief-signal-corruption.md), which replaces fx1's direction.
Each bust's secondary effects are now a kind of damaged video. The liquid wave, the scan, the rings and the grains are
gone. The mouths, rays, eyes, neck and layouts are as fx1 left them, apart from the jaw fix below.

Media: `~/Desktop/av-gen-review/92-talking-heads/fx2/` (every file is listed in the coordinator report).

## 1. Audit

What the renderer already had, and what each part could do:

| Capability | Exists? | Use here |
|---|---|---|
| Per-surface material programs (48 ops; world position, time, noise, `quantize` cells with per-cell hashes, thresholds, OKLab ramps) | yes | static, scanlines, macroblock colour loss, tracking-band dropouts, all entity-local by construction |
| World-space procedural deformers on every piece of a bust | yes (Bend, Twist, Sine, Hinge...) | none of them displaced whole cells, so Blocks was added (ADR-1674) |
| Duplicated geometry with alpha-blended programs (fx1's rim copies) | yes | RGB misregistration copies and VHS ghost frames |
| Previous-frame colour | full-screen only: the temporal ring (ADR-410) and its block mosh (ADR-1049) | NOT used. It has no entity mask, so it would be the full-screen VHS filter the brief rules out |
| Per-entity render targets or masks, motion vectors | no | would be a renderer-wide extension (an ID buffer, per-entity history). Not justified by the result |

The drum bust's fx1 treatment (abrupt sideways slices and stripes on each hit, gone in about 120 ms) was the part that
already worked. TRACKING FAILURE keeps it and makes it a real tear.

**Not true datamosh.** CODEC COLLAPSE is block corruption: whole macroblocks of the bust's geometry displaced, a
share of the blocks losing their image to flat smeared colour, and the pattern persisting and decaying over seconds. It
does not propagate motion vectors or re-use stale frames. True per-entity datamosh would need the missing per-entity
history, so it was not attempted. The persistence comes from slow envelopes, not frame history.

## 2. Engine changes (each Off is bit-identical, each with an ADR and tests)

- **ADR-1674: Blocks deformer (kind 9).** Displaces whole cells of a surface: cells `1/frequency` tall and
  `scale/frequency` wide. A share (`falloff`) of the cells, chosen by an integer hash (CPU = GPU) of cell, seed and epoch
  `floor(phase)`, moves by `amount x (2h - 1)` along `displacementAxis`. `speed` is a still band: the displacement ramps
  from 0 at `center` to full `speed` away along `axis`. The rays ride the head's node, which no deformer moves, so the
  mouth stays nearly put while the forehead and chest break up. Amount 0 or share 0 is identity.
- **ADR-1675: thirty-two material program slots** (was 16). Four busts with their copies need 21 programs. **This also
  found an fx1 defect:** fx1 had 19 programs, so th4's porcelain (the grain dissolve) and th3's second copy were never on
  the GPU. Liveness flagged them as dead routes, but nothing in the picture said so. The update now writes only the
  header and the programs in use.
- **ADR-1672 addendum: the Hinge mask eases** (smoothstep from a quarter of the radius), so the turned jaw meets the
  still face without a crease.

## 3. The profiles

All four are entity-local: material programs on the bust's own pieces, world-space deformers on the same pieces, and
copies parented to the head. Every route reads only its own slot's signals: `<P>LevelS` (the slot's band level with
the sensitivity knob), `<P>Onset` (its onset strength) and the gate's `Open`/`Live`. There is no new analysis.

**TRACKING FAILURE** (default: drums). The **Hit** event: onsets over 0.6, falling at 7/s, so about 140 ms.
- Blocks bands 2.4 cm tall and full width: up to 55% of them thrown sideways by up to 4.5 cm, with a fresh pattern every
  few frames while the hit lasts. Decay 90-150 ms.
- Strips of the surface drop out to animated monochrome static (hashed bands; time-run noise).
- Red and cyan copies jump out of register by 1.6 cm and flash.
- The fx1 shake.
- Clean between hits.

**CODEC COLLAPSE** (default: bass).
- Macroblocks about 11 cm square (9 per metre, scale 0.85).
- The smoothed level (the bass band) sets the share displaced (up to 28%) and how far (2.2 cm). It rises over 350-500 ms
  and clears over 2.5-3 s.
- A **Crash** (onsets over 1.3, a 2 s decay) adds up to 30% more share and 3 cm more distance, and reshuffles the block
  pattern once (**Jump**, about 80 ms).
- Blocks lose their image to flat colour from the palette with per-row streaks (the smear).
- A sustained bass holds the bust visibly broken. A strong hit is a reconstruction failure that takes seconds to heal.

**BROADCAST INTERFERENCE** (default: synth). No geometry moves.
- Patches of animated static open as the level holds: a 900 ms rise and a 2.2 s fall, with the threshold drifting
  down. The patches drift with the held level.
- Inside a patch the static obscures the surface (it replaces the base colour, not just a glow).
- Scanlines in the palette's colour shimmer line by line round the patches, never as stripes over the whole bust.
- Red and blue copies drift apart over about a second.
- An onset (**Burst**: onsets over 1.0, held 40 ms, about 350 ms) bursts all three.

**TEMPORAL GHOSTING** (default: the fourth source). The bust itself stays clean.
- Two faint whole copies of the head, each its own opacity (floors 0.5 and 0.34 of the gain) and colour, sit a little
  out of place while the source sounds.
- An onset (**Sep**: about 0.9 s) throws them apart (4.5 and 3 cm, turned 7 and 5 degrees), and they drift back.
- The copies are frames of the head at rest. They do not open their mouths, and they fade out round the mouth (the
  closed lip slit caught the rim light as a dash).

**Configurable.** A slot's profile is the `profile` key in the scene's slot table (`HEADS`), not tied to the role. Any
slot can take any of the four, and a missing or unknown profile takes `DEFAULT_PROFILE` (tracking). CC 4 (effects) and CC 5
(tearing) scale them, as before.

## 4. Carried over from fx1's notes

- **Eyes and mouth attached.** The eyeballs are split into white and iris procs. The eyes, lids and the mouth's cavity
  are procedural pieces carrying the head's own surface deformers, with the same values and routes. Whatever moves the
  face carries them. `close/*-face.mp4` and their sheets show each profile at its loud passage, close on the face.
- **The seam under the chin.** The extended check found it. With the mouth open and the neck bent back 32 degrees, the
  dropping jaw folded the under-chin into a dark pocket: the hinge mask's ease ran over 3 cm while the chin swung 2.5 cm.
  - The mask is now r 0.10, with its centre raised so its lowest point is unchanged, still above the head piece's
    bottom.
  - The pocket is gone (`chin/before...` vs `chin/after...`).
  - One small dot remains where the lip taper meets the jaw's corner. It shows only from below the chin in the
    three-quarter view, at full opening and full tilt.
- **seam_check.py** renders each head alone in four views: front, three-quarter, jaw-side and jaw-three-quarter (mouth
  open, tilt 32, camera under the chin).
  - The piece-seam step is scored against its surroundings.
  - On the jaw views, the under-chin path from chin to neck is scored against the same view with the mouth closed.
  - The Blocks close-ups are written but not scored (their own cell edges are steps by design).
  - It is kept as the regression check.
- **No drop shadow.** None was reintroduced. Copies are never black.
- **The 1-4 slot system** was audited and left as it was. `lifecycle.txt` and `isolation.txt` pass.

- Within the 30 fps budget (33.3 ms) at every count, with 10 ms of GPU headroom at p95 with four busts.

  four (the corruption programs' fragments and the alpha copies). Depth, AO and post are flat.
- fx1's four-bust figure was low partly because th4's programs never ran (ADR-1675).

## 6. Not done, and limits

- **No true temporal datamosh** (see the audit). There is no per-entity frame history.
- **The copies don't deform.** A procedural copy ignored its program's opacity and drew solid, so the copies stay gltf
  frames of the head at rest. A fix would make procedural pieces honour a program's opacity, which is an engine change
  left for the owner to want.
- **The small dot at the jaw's corner** from below (section 4).
- **Runtime profile choice** is the slot table, not yet a MIDI control.
