# Prototype — DIGITAL ALPINE

Replace the Particle / VFX World prototype with a **stylized digital mountain-and-water environment**.

This should be one of the most visually beautiful prototypes in Sonic Garden, but it should achieve that beauty through **composition, shape, atmosphere, color and motion**, not through lots of expensive geometry or flashy effects.

## Core Concept

Create a vast stylized mountain landscape surrounding a body of water.

The environment should feel like a **digital painting that has been turned into a navigable 3D world**.

The mountains should be simple, elegant forms.

The water should provide a large reflective/animated surface.

The atmosphere should create enormous depth.

The overall aesthetic should be:

* sophisticated
* minimal
* atmospheric
* colorful
* slightly surreal
* graphic
* cinematic
* clearly digital

But **not realistic** and absolutely **not cheap-looking 3D graphics**.

---

# Visual Direction

Think:

> a beautifully designed low-poly/digital landscape viewed from a moving camera

rather than:

> a realistic mountain simulator.

Use simple mountain silhouettes with strong shapes.

Mountains can be:

* smooth faceted forms
* layered ridges
* sculpted low-poly surfaces
* graphic silhouettes
* stylized stepped terrain
* simple procedural meshes

Do not cover them in detailed rock textures.

The **silhouette should do most of the work.**

---

# Water

Water should be a major visual element.

Use a large lake, ocean, or river basin occupying a substantial portion of the composition.

The water can be:

* glassy
* softly rippled
* reflective
* stylized
* subtly emissive
* color-shifting

Avoid obvious procedural water effects.

No giant cartoon waves.

No cheesy glowing ocean.

Instead, use subtle animated surface structure and beautiful reflections.

The water should act as a huge visual canvas reflecting the mountain silhouettes and sky.

---

# Camera

The camera should **fly through the landscape**.

Possible movements:

* low flight over the water
* sweeping between mountain valleys
* passing close to a shoreline
* rising gradually to reveal a huge basin
* flying toward distant mountains
* skimming across water toward a central peak
* banking around a mountain and revealing a new vista

Movement should be smooth and deliberate.

Avoid frantic camera motion.

The landscape should provide the sense of movement.

---

# Composition

Every shot should have strong spatial layering:

### Foreground

Water, shoreline, rocks, vegetation or silhouetted terrain.

### Midground

Large mountain forms, islands, ridges, waterfalls or stylized structures.

### Background

Distant mountains disappearing into atmospheric haze.

### Far background

A restrained sky/color field.

This should create a strong:

**foreground → water → mountains → atmosphere → horizon**

relationship.

The environment should feel expansive.

---

# Digital Art Treatment

The landscape should feel digital without becoming tacky.

Explore restrained techniques such as:

* flat/faceted shading
* subtle color banding
* stylized atmospheric gradients
* controlled edge lighting
* simplified materials
* gentle color transitions
* selective emissive accents
* subtle geometric contour lines
* occasional graphic overlays

Do NOT cover the landscape in:

* wireframe grids
* glowing outlines
* rainbow gradients
* neon edges
* floating particles everywhere
* excessive bloom

The digital quality should come from the **design language**, not a collection of effects.

---

# Color

Use strong but sophisticated palettes.

Possible directions:

### Blue / violet

Deep blue water, violet mountains, pale atmospheric horizon.

### Teal / coral

Cool landscape with restrained warm accents.

### Indigo / gold

Deep blue environment with warm light reflected across the water.

### Emerald / violet

Dark green terrain with violet atmospheric light.

### Sunset gradient

Warm horizon transitioning into increasingly cool mountains and water.

Do not use every color simultaneously.

Each scene state should have a coherent palette.

---

# Optional Stylized Elements

Small amounts of highly controlled environmental detail can include:

* simple geometric vegetation
* isolated luminous plants
* crystalline formations
* waterfalls
* distant islands
* thin mist layers
* small structures
* floating seeds/leaves
* subtle environmental particles

These should reinforce the composition.

They should NOT become the primary visual language.

---

# Audio Reactivity

This environment should be extremely responsive without looking like a visualizer.

## Bass

Use bass for large environmental movement:

* water swell
* subtle mountain deformation
* large-scale lighting
* atmospheric pulse
* shoreline illumination
* reflection intensity

The mountains should **breathe very subtly** rather than visibly bouncing.

---

## Midrange

Use mids for:

* water ripples
* changing terrain illumination
* atmospheric movement
* vegetation movement
* color transitions
* flowing light through valleys

---

## High Frequencies

Use highs for small-scale details:

* water sparkle
* tiny highlights
* distant lights
* subtle particles
* fine contour activity

Keep these restrained.

---

## Onsets

Strong transients can create:

* concentric ripples across water
* a wave of illumination traveling through the mountain range
* a temporary color shift across the landscape
* brief reflections appearing on the water
* distant atmospheric flashes

These should feel like the landscape is responding organically to the music.

---

## Spectral Energy

Different frequency bands can illuminate different mountain ranges.

For example:

* bass → foreground terrain
* low mids → central mountains
* mids → distant ridges
* highs → atmosphere/sky

This creates a **depth-based audio response** rather than simply making everything pulse.

---

## Tempo

Tempo can control:

* water movement
* slow environmental oscillations
* traveling light patterns
* camera movement
* subtle atmospheric cycles

---

# Signature Feature — Audio Topography

Explore the idea that **sound creates temporary topography.**

For example, a strong musical passage could cause subtle waves of deformation to travel through the mountain landscape.

Not cartoonishly.

Not:

> mountains bouncing to the beat.

Instead:

> a smooth deformation wave travels across the terrain like sound moving through a physical landscape.

The same concept could travel across the water as expanding ripples.

This gives the scene a unique Sonic Garden identity.

---

# Performance Requirements

This scene should be designed specifically for live rendering.

Prefer:

* low-poly terrain
* relatively low mesh counts
* instancing
* simple materials
* simple lighting
* shader-based deformation where practical
* procedural water
* atmospheric effects that are inexpensive
* limited particle counts

Do not solve visual quality by increasing geometry density.

A beautifully shaped 5,000-polygon mountain is preferable to a 500,000-polygon realistic mountain.

---

# What This Should NOT Become

Do not make:

* a realistic mountain simulator
* a low-poly asset-store landscape
* a Tron landscape
* a wireframe mountain range
* a neon fantasy world
* a generic game environment
* a glowing particle landscape
* a terrain demo

The target is:

> **stylized digital landscape art with exceptional composition, atmosphere and audio responsiveness.**

It should look sophisticated even when absolutely nothing is moving.

Then the audio should make the entire landscape subtly come alive.

---

# Quality Test

Disable all audio modulation.

Freeze the camera.

Look at the resulting frame.

It should already look like a compelling piece of digital artwork.

Then enable the audio.

The landscape should become:

* alive
* responsive
* atmospheric
* subtly transformative

without losing its visual elegance.

The viewer should feel:

> **"I'm flying through a beautiful digital mountain world that is listening to the music."**

Not:

> **"A bunch of graphics are reacting to a song."**
