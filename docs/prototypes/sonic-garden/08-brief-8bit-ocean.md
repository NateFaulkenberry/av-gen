# Prototype — 8-BIT OCEAN

Create a stylized underwater world inspired by the visual language and joyful atmosphere of classic 8-bit / 16-bit platformer water levels.

The inspiration should be:

* colorful underwater platforming
* playful game environments
* simple iconic shapes
* bright aquatic colors
* oversized environmental elements
* bubbles
* sea plants
* coral
* floating particles
* friendly strange creatures
* simple ruins
* strong silhouettes
* layered depth

Do **not** attempt to make a realistic underwater environment.

Do **not** create realistic coral, realistic fish, realistic ocean floors, or photorealistic water.

The goal is:

> **A beautiful 3D audiovisual interpretation of an old-school pixel-art water level.**

---

# Visual Style

Use deliberately simple geometry.

Think:

* chunky shapes
* low-poly forms
* flat shading
* hard color transitions
* simple silhouettes
* saturated colors
* exaggerated proportions
* graphic materials
* simplified lighting

The scene should feel like someone took an iconic 8-bit/16-bit underwater game world and expanded it into a navigable 3D audiovisual space.

It should be **charming and visually rich**, not technically realistic.

---

# Environment

Create a coherent underwater level rather than an open empty ocean.

The player/camera should travel through an environment containing:

* colorful sea floor
* coral formations
* large stylized sea plants
* rock formations
* simple underwater ruins
* arches
* columns
* treasure-like objects
* bubbles
* small creatures
* larger background silhouettes
* floating particles

Use exaggerated scale.

A flower-like underwater plant could be enormous.

A shell could be the size of a house.

A tiny creature could establish scale against it.

The environment should feel like a **game level designed by an artist**, not an ocean simulation.

---

# Spatial Composition

Maintain strong environmental depth.

### Foreground

Large plants, coral, bubbles and rocks passing close to the camera.

### Midground

The primary playable-looking environment:

* paths
* arches
* plants
* ruins
* creatures
* coral

### Background

Large simplified silhouettes:

* giant coral
* distant ruins
* rock walls
* enormous plants
* schools of tiny creatures

Use atmospheric blue depth to separate layers.

The camera should have a clear direction of travel.

---

# Camera

The camera should slowly **swim/fly through the level**.

Possible sequences:

* entering through a coral arch
* swimming through a dense plant field
* passing a giant ruin
* traveling between enormous rocks
* descending toward a colorful basin
* swimming through a tunnel
* emerging into a larger open chamber

The motion should feel playful and smooth.

Do not use aggressive cinematic camera movement.

This should feel like **exploring a game level**.

---

# Color

Color is extremely important.

Use bold, saturated game-like colors:

* deep ocean blue
* cyan
* turquoise
* purple
* pink
* orange
* yellow
* lime green
* coral red

But organize them into intentional palettes.

For example:

### Area 1

Blue + cyan + yellow

### Area 2

Purple + pink + turquoise

### Area 3

Deep blue + orange + green

The scene can transition between palettes as the camera moves.

Avoid muddy realistic underwater coloration.

---

# Water

The water should be represented primarily through **stylization**, not physical simulation.

Use:

* blue atmospheric depth
* soft volumetric haze
* subtle caustic patterns
* drifting particles
* bubbles
* gentle light rays
* animated color gradients

Avoid expensive realistic underwater rendering.

The viewer should immediately understand:

**we are underwater**

without needing realistic water physics.

---

# Retro Game Language

Explore visual elements that feel like they came from a classic game:

* blocky rock shapes
* chunky coral
* simple coins/gems/collectibles as environmental accents
* pipes or tubes
* arches
* columns
* platforms
* stylized ruins
* oversized flowers
* simple geometric creatures
* repeating decorative patterns

Do not literally copy recognizable Mario assets.

Create original assets that evoke the **visual language** of classic platformer games.

---

# 8-Bit Interpretation

There are several ways to evoke 8-bit aesthetics.

Explore:

* very simple materials
* flat shading
* limited palettes
* deliberately low-poly models
* hard color transitions
* pixel-inspired textures
* posterized lighting
* simple silhouettes
* occasional pixel-art-inspired particles

Do not force a pixelation filter over the entire scene.

The result should look like:

> **3D art designed by someone who loves 8-bit games**

rather than:

> modern 3D graphics with a pixelation shader slapped on top.

---

# Audio Reactivity

This environment should be extremely playful and responsive.

## Bass

Bass can control:

* large plant movement
* coral pulse
* gentle sea-floor deformation
* large bubble size
* large environmental lighting

Keep movement rhythmic and organic.

---

## Midrange

Mids can control:

* plant swaying
* creature movement
* bubble streams
* coral color
* environmental lights
* small object movement

---

## High Frequencies

Highs can control:

* tiny bubbles
* sparkling particles
* small creatures
* highlights
* little bursts of light
* small coral details

---

## Onsets

Strong musical transients can trigger:

* bubble bursts
* coral blooms
* schools of tiny creatures changing direction
* brief color flashes
* treasure/gem illumination
* particle bursts
* expanding underwater rings

Make these feel playful.

---

## Tempo

Tempo can control:

* plant sway
* creature swimming cycles
* bubble streams
* environmental pulses
* camera swimming speed

---

# MIDI Interaction

Make this environment particularly responsive to MIDI.

Possible mappings:

### Notes

Individual notes could:

* illuminate coral
* spawn bubbles
* trigger creature movement
* activate plants
* create small particle trails

### Pitch

Pitch could control:

* vertical position
* hue
* depth
* plant size

### Velocity

Velocity could control:

* bubble burst size
* brightness
* creature speed
* particle intensity

### Chords

Chords could trigger:

* large coral blooms
* synchronized environmental lighting
* schools of creatures
* large bubble formations

### Sustained notes

Sustained notes could maintain:

* glowing plants
* illuminated ruins
* persistent bubble streams
* active environmental areas

---

# Signature Visual Feature — The Living Level

The environment should feel like the **entire game level is reacting to the music.**

For example:

A bass hit causes several giant sea plants to gently bend.

A melody causes individual flowers/coral formations to illuminate sequentially.

A chord causes an entire area of coral to bloom.

A drum transient releases a swarm of bubbles.

High-frequency activity creates tiny sparkling creatures.

The result should feel playful and musical.

---

# Performance

This should be deliberately inexpensive to render.

Prefer:

* low-poly meshes
* instancing
* simple materials
* flat shading
* simple lighting
* lightweight particles
* shader-driven animation
* procedural placement

Do not solve visual complexity with expensive geometry.

The scene should demonstrate that **simple assets + excellent composition + excellent color + animation can look fantastic.**

---

# Avoid

Do NOT make:

* realistic ocean scenery
* realistic coral
* realistic fish
* realistic underwater ruins
* photorealistic caustics
* generic aquarium graphics
* a dark horror ocean
* endless blue fog with random objects
* neon cyberpunk underwater
* a pixelation filter covering mediocre 3D

The target is:

> **playful, colorful, stylized, nostalgic, beautiful and highly reactive.**

---

# Final Visual Target

Imagine taking the best qualities of an iconic classic video-game water level:

* instantly readable shapes
* saturated colors
* charming creatures
* memorable environments
* simple geometry
* strong silhouettes
* playful motion

and translating that into a sophisticated modern 3D audiovisual experience.

The scene should be fun to look at **before the music starts**.

Then the music should make the entire underwater world come alive.

The goal is not:

> "Look how realistically we rendered an ocean."

The goal is:

> **"This feels like a lost, gorgeous underwater game level that somehow became a living audiovisual instrument."**
