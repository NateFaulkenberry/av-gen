# Liminal Euclidean World: director plan for *All You Got*

*The art agent, 2026-09-30. This is the §13 plan for the music video. It follows the owner's brief
(`00-brief.md`) and the owner's emotional addendum (`01-addendum-emotion.md`), which wins where they differ. The
music is analysed in `SONG-ANALYSIS.md` and the section map is `tools/liminal/all-you-got.sections.json`. The
research behind the choices is in `ART-RESEARCH.md`, cited here as "rule 1-10". The engineering design it builds on
is `ENGINEERING.md`. The needs from engineering are at the end, ranked.*

## 1. The idea

> **Someone is lost in an enormous, empty, beautiful building whose rooms connect wrongly. They keep walking,
> trying to make sense of themselves, and in the end the building opens and they walk out into the light.**

The song keeps saying "steps in the process", and each time the second half changes: breathe and grow, feel and
grow, let it go, heal and grow, feel it grow, for your life. **The film is that walk.** One continuous journey with
no cuts, at walking pace, through a building that is ordinary in every part and impossible as a whole.

Three things from the song's own structure shape the whole plan (`SONG-ANALYSIS.md`):

1. **The song has three textures, and the film has three places.**
   - **The sparse family** (intro, break, "is that all you", outro) is the **first corridor**, where we start and
     where the loop brings us back.
   - **The C-Dorian groove** (the dance section, both verses, the bridge, "is that all?") is **the building**:
     halls, rooms, stairs.
   - **The F world** ("feel it grow", the final chorus, the call and response) is **the open**: daylight, sky,
     the building unfolding.
2. **"Feel it grow" is a preview of the ending**: the same key and texture as the last chorus, 55 s early. So
   the open world is **glimpsed through a doorway at "feel it grow"** and **entered at the release** (rule 8: the
   release is first seen through a doorway).
3. **"Is that all you" returns to the intro's texture.** So the loop section takes place **in the first corridor
   again**, recognisably the same and one thing different. The viewer's "Have I been here before?" is answered by
   the song: yes.

The governing test for every shot is the owner's: *does this make the viewer feel something, or merely
demonstrate something the renderer can do?* The emotional line is the addendum's:

> quiet, curiosity, energy, intimacy, silence, growth, tension, release

That line is a series of changes of kind, never a ramp of intensity.

## 2. The world: a strange dream of an ordinary building

**What it is made of.**
- Plain plaster walls and flat ceilings.
- Doorways that are openings, with no doors: a simple reveal and a thin frame.
- Straight stair flights with real risers (17 cm) and flat landings, and sometimes a single plain handrail.
- Tall plain window openings, a dark skirting line at the base of the walls, and floors laid in two alternating
  bands.

All of these are human-scale cues that keep the mood melancholy rather than hostile (rule 1; NaissanceE's
lesson). **Nothing else**: no columns, arches, mouldings, panels, pipes, screens or signage.

**How it is wrong.**
- **Proportion.** Corridors are 5 m tall where 2.7 m would be normal. Doorways are 3.2 m tall and narrow. Rooms are
  sometimes much too large (a 40 m hall with nothing in it).
- **Connection.** A stair returns to the corridor it left. A doorway opens onto the room you just walked out of.
  A window looks into a room from an angle that cannot exist.
- **Light.** Sun falls where no sun can reach (Hopper), and a warm light always waits in a doorway far away.

Every part is plausible locally, and the whole is impossible (Escher's Relativity, rule 7). Impossibilities are
**shown in one view** so they register: through a doorway, down a stairwell, across a void. They are never made
out of sight, where Suma's study shows nobody notices them.

**Space and emptiness.** Every room has one idea (Ueda's design by subtraction). Negative space is a material:
- large floors;
- distant walls fading into **luminous** air;
- voids beyond landings;
- windows that show only fog until the end.

The air is lighter than the walls, so depth reads as aerial perspective. The POC's look is kept (fog that swallows
distance, light in the air, fogged edge lines, one restrained accent) but turned from a dark violet void into a pale
dream. The addendum forbids "oppressive darkness for its own sake".

**Edges.** Keep the POC's edge term, but thin and pencil-like: a faint graphite or warm line on creases that fades
with fog. Escher's prints are line drawings, and the line makes plain geometry read as drawn architecture rather
than grey blocks. It is never cyan and never neon.

## 3. The motifs

Four recurring things, each ordinary at first and then found in steadily more impossible places. The first two carry
the film; the figure and the sun patch support them.

**1. The lit doorway (the beacon).**
- **What it is:** a doorway far away with warm light spilling out of it. In the grey opening it is the one warm
  thing. It is Journey's mountain (rule 2): wherever the camera wanders, it keeps finding this light, so a viewer
  with the sound off knows there is somewhere to reach.
- **What it means:** the way forward, always one room away.
- **Its arc:**
  - at the end of the first corridor (ordinary);
  - high in the wall of the great hall (unreachable);
  - through a stairwell, below us (we were above it, somehow);
  - at the end of every room in the enfilade;
  - across the void at "let it go", faint;
  - opening onto the sky at "feel it grow" (the first glimpse of the open);
  - at the end of the loop corridor that never gets closer ("is that all you");
  - finally passed through at the release, the downbeat of bar 92;
  - then everywhere at once in the open, many doorways standing free and all lit.

**2. The staircase.**
- **What it is:** one ordinary flight with a landing and a single rail, Escher's device. It is always the same stair
  design, so it is recognisable.
- **Its arc:**
  - climbed in the intro, where it arrives somewhere it cannot (the first wrongness);
  - seen from above across the great hall, crossing other flights;
  - descended into fog at "let it go", because quietening reads as descent (rule 9);
  - flights crossing at contradictory levels in verse 2;
  - a square stairwell climbed forever in "is that all?", four flights up and back at the same landing, which is
    the Penrose loop made of real topology;
  - at the release, a stair that starts indoors and ends in open air (Escher's Belvedere);
  - in the open, stairs standing free and leading up.

**3. The figure (optional).**
- **What it is:** a tiny human silhouette, 1.75 m tall, always seen from behind, always standing still (Friedrich's
  Rückenfigur, rule 6). It gives scale and someone to identify with. It is never approached closely, never turned to
  face us, and never used as a threat.
- **Where it appears:**
  - at the far end of the great hall at bar 18, which makes the hall's size legible in one frame;
  - two rooms ahead in the enfilade (verse 1); a doorframe passes across the lens, and when it clears the figure is
    gone, so the camera goes on alone;
  - at the top of the stair facing the fog at "let it go" (bar 42), where the camera stops behind it; when the
    pulse returns the camera takes another flight down and leaves it above;
  - **absent** from "feel it grow" to the end of the loop, where the loneliness is at its height;
  - at the release, silhouetted at the top of the open stair against the sky. The camera climbs towards it and, at
    bar 108, arrives beside it and **takes over its gaze** onto the whole building laid out below. We find the one
    who knew the way, or we become them.
- **Fallback:** if a figure cannot be made to look right, the scale comes from the environment instead: door heights,
  risers, rails and the skirting line.

**4. The sun patch.**
- **What it is:** a rectangle of warm sunlight on a wall or floor, in rooms with no window facing the sun (Hopper,
  rule 5). It is the gentlest impossibility.
- **Its arc:**
  - it first appears in the intro, as the first thing that is subtly wrong;
  - in the stripped "let it go" groove it slides slowly across the floor, the only moving thing, like time passing;
  - it is the one small change in each pass of the loop.

## 4. The camera: a wanderer

The camera is the lost person (rule 1): Russian Ark's narrator who does not know where he is. It is never a drone
and never a roller coaster.

- **Height and speed.** Eye height (1.6 m), at walking pace. At 109 BPM the song is already a walking cadence of
  about 1.8 steps a second; the step up to 111 at bar 76 is a walker quickening. Speeds by section are in §7. The
  fastest is 2.2 m/s, a brisk walk. The slowest is standing still.
- **The grammar of uncertainty**, all authored on the timeline:
  - **holds**, where it stops to look;
  - **hesitations**, a small backward drift of 20-40 cm before going on;
  - **looking around**, a slow yaw toward a side doorway, then back;
  - **turning for no clear reason**, the path taking a side door at a phrase end;
  - **entering rooms without knowing what is inside**, where the doorway's reveal fills the frame for a moment
    before the room resolves.
- **Symmetry is earned.** One-point perspective down the axis of a space is kept for confidence: the dance section
  and the release. When it is lost, the camera drifts off axis with a slow gaze sway.
- **The walk is felt, not shown.** A bob of a centimetre or two, stepping on the beat, and only while walking.
  Never a shake, never an impulse on a kick.
- **The one impossible move.** At the release it rises through the stairwell and out through where the ceiling
  was (Enter the Void, Soy Cuba). Everything else is a walk.
- **No cuts.** Where the world must change structurally, the camera passes through a **threshold of light**: a
  doorway whose reveal fills the frame, or a brightening of the air as when stepping into sunlight. The swap happens
  there (Rope, Birdman and 1917 hide their joins the same way; rule 7). These thresholds are bright, not black: this
  is a dream, not a cut.
- **The doorway is the unit of rhythm** (rule 4). Rooms and bays are sized so the camera crosses a threshold on
  phrase downbeats, never on every beat:
  - in verse 1, at 1.1 m/s, a threshold every 4 bars (8.8 s) makes a room about 9.7 m long;
  - in the loop corridor at 2.0 m/s, ceiling lights every 2.16 m pass overhead on every other beat. The
    eighth-note pulse becomes something the space does as we move through it, not a flash (Star Guitar).

## 5. The colour script

Colour follows the addendum's emotional story:
- cool and muted at first;
- deep saturated colours appearing with the first energy;
- receding in the breakdown;
- returning and spreading at "feel it grow";
- one constrained hue in the tension;
- opening at the release.

Four craft rules from the research (rule 8):

1. **Build it additively on grey.** Each chapter adds one hue family (GRIS).
2. **Key chroma and lightness separately.** Chroma is energy and arousal; lightness is mood and valence (Valdez
   and Mehrabian). Nothing is black-dominant.
3. **Withhold one quality: daylight.** Clear sky blue and warm white sun appear nowhere in the building. Windows show
   only fog, and all light is lamps and the beacon until the doorway at "feel it grow" frames it. It fills the world
   only at bar 92 (WALL-E's green, Oz's door). The known failure is leakage, so every emitter is policed (fog, sky,
   bounce, emissives, grade).
4. **Use two timescales** (Scriabin). The slow voice is about ten palette states, moved by `palette/position`. The
   fast voice is a drift of a few degrees of fog hue that follows the chord each bar: Cm cooler, F warmer, smoothed
   over a bar. It is felt as life, not seen as change.

Transitions are slow except at three places, and each of those rides a musical event:
- bar 18, a two-bar crossfade from the gap into the groove;
- bar 42, the colour draining over the bar;
- bar 92, the doorway crossing, where the palette opens over one bar as the camera passes through.

Everywhere else the palette moves over four to eight bars.

## 6. Light

- **Intro:** flat, grey daylight from tall windows (Hammershøi), low contrast, and a warm beacon far away.
- **Dance:** the first **shafts**. Volumetric light through high windows into the hall, with motes hanging in it.
- **Verse:** **lamp pools**, one per room (Kentucky Route Zero), the windows turning to dusk.
- **"Let it go":** dim and even. The beacon fades almost out. The sun patch slides.
- **"Feel it grow":** **light spreads**. The beacon's radius and intensity grow bar by bar, and the gold reaches
  further into the room each phrase: growth as light filling a space (the addendum's "light spreading", "colour
  slowly filling a space").
- **"Is that all you":** a line of sodium ceiling lights down the corridor. Their spacing makes them pass overhead
  in time with the pulse at the camera's speed.
- **Release:** daylight. A sun, a sky and long soft shadows. The first directional light that is not a lamp.
- **Outro:** over-bright, even haze.

Bloom stays near the POC's 0.25 at threshold 1, and there is no grain.

## 7. The film, section by section

Times are in seconds and bars (`SONG-ANALYSIS.md`). "Speed" is the camera's walking speed. "Coupling" is how
strongly the music may move the world, from 0 to 1. It follows the contrast line, not a ramp (rule 9; Basanta's
coupling as modulation).

---

### 1. Intro, bars 1-17 (0.00-37.43 s): *"This place does not obey ordinary space."*

**Music:**
- 2.2 s of silence;
- a sparse two-bar call and response;
- the bass pulse at bar 6 (11.01 s);
- the riser from bar 13;
- the "all you got" chop from 30.5 s;
- a one-beat gap on bar 17 beat 4 (36.88 s).

**What we see.**
- **Silence (0-2.2 s):** the film opens in stillness, looking down a plain, pale, too-tall corridor in grey
  daylight. Nothing moves; the first bar is silent, and so is the picture. The corridor has doorways on both sides
  opening into unlit rooms, tall windows full of fog on the left, and far away at its end a doorway with warm light
  in it.
- **The walk begins (bar 2):** when the music starts, the camera begins to walk. It is slow and unsure, and it
  pauses at the first side doorway to look into an empty room.
- **The first wrongness (bar 6):** when the bass pulse enters, a patch of warm sunlight lies on the corridor
  floor, though the windows face only fog. It is the first thing that is wrong, and it is small.
- **The loop (bars 8-12):** the corridor ends at a stair going up. The camera climbs it, turns on the landing,
  climbs again, and arrives at the top in **the same corridor**, seen from its far end. The sun patch and the
  beacon are there, now behind it.
  - This is the "map move" (Panic Room) that teaches the space, and the first impossibility, shown in one view: the
    stair we just climbed is visible through a doorway **below** us.
  - It is unhurried and not dramatic. The viewer should half-notice it.
- **The riser (bars 13-17):** the camera finds a second stair, wider, rising towards brighter light. With the
  riser it climbs a little faster, and the light ahead grows. At the top, on the gap (bar 17 beat 4), it stops for
  one beat on the threshold.

**Camera:** 0.5-0.8 m/s, with holds and one hesitation. **Architecture:** still, with no deformation. **Colour:**
K0. **Coupling:** 0.15. The riser brightens the light ahead, and nothing else answers the music.

**Why:** the addendum asks the intro to start almost mundane and reveal the wrongness subtly. The music is
cinematic and wide here (the widest stereo image in the song), so the picture is space and light, not event. The
stair that returns to its own corridor seeds both motifs (the stair, the beacon) and the question "Have I been here
before?".

---

### 2. Dance section, bars 18-25 (37.43-55.05 s): *"The world suddenly feels alive."*

**Music:** the full groove on the downbeat (sub-bass, a syncopated kick, offbeat hats) with the "all you got" chop
once a bar.

**What we see.**
- **The reveal (bar 18):** on the downbeat the camera steps out onto a landing high on the wall of **the great
  hall**. The room is 40 m long and 20 m high, and empty.
  - Light pours down through high windows in slanted shafts full of motes, and one long wall is a single plane of
    deep rose.
  - Far below and across, **the figure** stands small on another landing, back to us, looking up at the beacon
    high in the far wall. Its size makes the hall's size legible in one frame.
- **The stairs (bars 18-25):** stairs cross the hall at different heights, all ordinary, all with the same gravity,
  some leading to doorways in mid-wall. The camera goes down the long stair into the hall with confidence and on
  the axis. It crosses the floor towards the far wall, and the hall's proportions breathe slowly with the bass.
- **Bar 25:** it reaches a doorway under the beacon as the phrase ends.

**Camera:** 1.4-1.7 m/s, symmetric and assured, with one long look up at the shafts. **Architecture:** the bass
breathing begins. A very low-frequency warp and ±1.5 % of room scale make the space heave slowly under the low end,
through a slow spring (rule 9: bass is large, heavy and slow). **Colour:** K0 → K1 over the first two bars.
**Coupling:** 0.7.

**Why:** "The world suddenly feels alive," not "more geometry". Energy is expressed as scale, light, colour and
confidence, while the loneliness stays: one tiny figure in an empty hall. The "all you got" hook is a groove here,
not a question. The same words come back at bar 76 as a question, and the space will answer differently.

---

### 3. Verse 1, bars 26-41 (55.05-90.28 s): *intimacy; still wandering*

**Music:** the voice enters ("How little do I know?" three times, then "steps in the process, breathe and grow"),
over the same groove with fewer hats and a clear quarter-note pulse. From bar 34, "Come on, tell me what you
wanna", then "can you tell me it's fine though?", then "It's steps in a process, let it go" at 87.5 s.

**What we see.**
- **The enfilade:** a Hammershøi sequence of rooms of human size (6 by 9.7 m, 4 m high) joined by aligned
  doorways. Every view looks through two or three thresholds, each room lit by one lamp pool, and the windows turn
  to dusk.
  - The camera walks through at 1.1 m/s and crosses a threshold every four bars.
  - Each room is slightly different from the last (a little taller, a little narrower, the lamp on the other
    side), so "almost identical rooms" are felt rather than seen.
- **The figure (bars 30-37):** it stands two rooms ahead in a doorway's light. The camera follows. A doorframe
  crosses the lens, and when it clears, the figure is gone. The camera goes on alone.
- **The voice:** when the voice sings, the camera's gaze drifts towards the lit doorway ahead, and the beacon warms a
  little. When the voice rests, the gaze wanders to a side room. The voice steers attention, not geometry (Chion,
  rule 9).
- **Bars 34-41:** the pre-chorus is restless ("Come on, tell me what you wanna"). The camera takes a side door for
  no clear reason and finds a room identical to the one it left, beacon and all.
- **The stop:** on "let it go" (87.5-90.28 s) it comes to a landing and slows.

**Camera:** 1.0-1.2 m/s with a felt step on the beat, one hesitation per phrase, and the gaze following the voice.
**Architecture:** restrained. The breathing continues but smaller, and each room's proportions drift. **Colour:**
K1 → K2 over four bars. **Coupling:** 0.5.

**Why:** the addendum asks for the return to intimacy: easier to read, long rooms, recurring motifs, restrained
deformation. The pulse becomes the walker's steps, forward movement rather than impacts. Losing the figure here sets
up the loneliness that the rest of the film resolves.

---

### 4. "Let it go", bars 42-48 (90.28-105.69 s): *the world breathes out*

**Music:**
- **Bar 42 (90.28-92.48 s):** the bass and kick are out for exactly one bar under "let it go". The only real
  silence of the low end until bar 74.
- **Bars 43-48:** the kick and bass return alone, stripped and exposed. The bright synths are gone, and "let it
  go" repeats every half bar until about 99 s.

**What we see.**
- **Bar 42, everything stops.** The camera has come to the top of a long stair that descends into fog, and it
  stops. The world's breathing stops. The colour drains over the bar to grey-lilac. The beacon, across the void,
  fades almost out.
  - The figure stands at the top step ahead, back to us, facing an enormous empty space: a sea of fog with the
    faint lines of distant architecture (Friedrich).
  - **This is the one moment of absence** before the break, and it gets the whole bar.
- **Bars 43-48:** the pulse returns and the camera begins to go **down** a second flight, beside the figure's,
  leaving it above. It moves at half walking pace, as if carried by the pulse (quietening maps to descent, Eitan and
  Granot).
  - Nothing else moves except the sun patch, which slides slowly across a landing.
  - **Architecture stops fighting itself.** The slight lean and proportion drift of the earlier rooms settle to
    true: lines go plumb and still.
  - The repeated "let it go" does not trigger anything; the release is the stillness itself.

**Camera:** 0 m/s through bar 42, then 0.4-0.6 m/s descending, with a slow look back up at the figure at bar 46.
**Architecture:** warp 0; drift settling to true. **Colour:** K3 (the lowest chroma in the film). **Coupling:** 0
at bar 42, then 0.25 (only the camera's pace feels the pulse).

**Why:** the owner calls this "one of the most important visual moments in the entire video". The music's silence
is one bar long, so the picture's silence is that bar, placed exactly. The six stripped bars that follow are
intimacy, not silence, and the descent carries them. This is surrender: the first "let it go". Its meaning must be
unmistakably different from the second one at bar 92, which rises into light.

---

### 5. Rebuild and verse 2, bars 49-58 (105.69-127.71 s): *the wandering gets complicated*

**Music:**
- **Bars 49-50:** a held breath; the mids are gated, and the hats build.
- **Bar 51 (110.09 s):** verse 2, the full groove, and the densest lyric in the song ("do you wanna have fun? / as
  the fires keep burning / and the world stops turning…", "tell me you're the one / to make the world stop
  hurting…").

**What we see.**
- **Bars 49-50:** at the foot of the stair, the camera pauses in a narrow passage. The light ahead brightens in two
  steps with the build, and the colour begins to return.
- **From bar 51:** it walks into **the crossing stairwell**. Flights run at different levels and cross one another
  above and below, landings are joined by bridges, and doorways sit at every height.
  - It is the hall's vocabulary, denser and more contradictory: **more intersecting spaces, more repeated motifs,
    subtler contradictions** (the addendum).
  - A window in one wall looks into the enfilade from verse 1, from an angle that cannot exist, and the figure's
    lamp is still lit there.
  - The camera changes its mind twice, turning back a few steps and then taking another flight. Twice it sees the
    beacon below it through a stairwell.
- **Phrase ends:** a noise sweep closes each four-bar phrase (bars 54 and 58). There, and only there, the nearest
  surfaces tremble very slightly for a beat, like a surface under strain (rule 9: roughness is local).

**Camera:** 1.1-1.3 m/s, two reversals, and more yaw searching than in verse 1. **Architecture:** the breathing
returns. The flights' levels drift slowly, so what was level with us is a little lower when we look back.
**Colour:** K3 → K4 over the rebuild. **Coupling:** 0.6.

**Why:** "lyrically denser and somewhat more disheveled" is reflected as uncertainty and interconnection, not as
spectacle. The window into the earlier rooms is the film's second impossibility, shown in one view.

---

### 6. Bridge, bars 59-66 (127.71-145.32 s): *"How little do I know?" (x4); have I been here before?*

**Music:** the groove continues; "how little do I know?" four times, then "tell me it'll be fine though?", then
"steps in the process, feel it grow" at 143.3 s.

**What we see.**
- **The same enfilade** as verse 1, entered from the other end. The rooms, the doorways and the lamp pools are
  the same, **the same camera move** (path, height, speed) repeated, and **one thing different**: the light falls
  from the other side, and every room is a little larger than we remember.
  - The repeated move is the rhyme that tells the viewer it is a loop (Marienbad), and the repeated question in the
    lyric is the same rhyme in words.
- **The figure** is not there. Its doorway is empty.
- **Bars 63-66:** at the last room the beacon is closer than it has ever been. On "feel it grow" (143.3 s) the
  camera stops at the doorway of a very small room, about 3 by 3 m, with the beacon's light inside.

**Camera:** 1.1 m/s, the verse 1 path reversed and repeated. **Architecture:** as verse 1, with the rooms 10 %
larger. **Colour:** K5 (K2 again, the light flipped). **Coupling:** 0.5.

**Why:** the addendum's "Have I been here before?" is at its clearest here, because the song itself repeats its
opening question four times. Repetition, not bizarreness.

### 7. "Feel it grow", bars 67-73 (145.32-160.73 s): *the first hope*

**Music:** an impact on the downbeat of bar 67 (145.32 s), then the **key lifts from C to F**. The sub drops under
a sustained higher bass, the air brightens, the hats skip on the sixteenth pickups, and "[feel it grow]" repeats.
This is the texture of the ending, heard early.

**What we see.**
- **Bar 67:** on the impact the camera steps into the tiny room, and **the room begins to grow**.
  - Over seven bars its walls move away and its ceiling rises continuously, until the 3 m room is a 30 m hall. The
    camera keeps walking as it grows, so the far wall is never reached.
  - **Light spreads** from the beacon, which is here in the room at last as a warm source in the far wall. Its gold
    reaches further each phrase and fills the space, and the motes in it rise on the skipping hats.
- **Bars 70-73:** a doorway opens in the far wall as the room grows. Through it, for the first time in the film,
  we see **daylight: clear sky and white sun** (the withheld quality, rule 8).
  - We see it, and we do not reach it yet.

**Camera:** accelerating from 0.9 to 1.4 m/s, gaze level and steady on the far doorway: a clear direction for the
first time. **Architecture:** the room-growth transform (the one big continuous transformation before the release)
and gentle breathing. **Colour:** K5 → K6, the gold spreading from the source outwards (Pleasantville: local before
global). **Coupling:** 0.6.

**Why:** "Growth does not have to mean plants." It is a tiny room becoming enormous, light spreading and colour
slowly filling a space, all from the addendum's list. The music changes key here and previews the ending, so the
picture previews the ending too: the open, seen through a door.

### 8. Break, bars 74-75 (160.73-165.14 s): *the held breath*

**Music:** drums out and sub out: sustained pads and one bass note. It is not quiet; the pulse is absent.

**What we see.** The camera reaches the far doorway and **stops in it**, looking out into the light. For two bars
nothing moves: no breathing, no motes rising, the camera still. The sky glows through the opening, and the gold
holds.

**Camera:** 0 m/s. **Architecture:** still. **Colour:** K7 (K6 held, 10 % darker). **Coupling:** 0.

**Why:** the addendum says silence should produce "actual absence of activity". This is the song's only
drums-out passage, and the stillest four seconds of the film. It is also the cruellest: the camera is on the
threshold of the open when the tempo changes.

### 9. "Is that all you", bars 76-83 (165.14-182.43 s): *the loop*

**Music:** **the tempo steps up to 111** on the downbeat of bar 76. A driving eighth-note low pulse, no sub, the
darkest top in the song, building bar by bar. "Is that all you" four times, "is that all you got?" twice. The pulse
stops on bar 83 beat 3 (181.35 s).

**What we see.**
- **Bar 76:** the camera steps through the doorway, and **it is the first corridor again**: the one from the
  intro, recognisable by its tall windows, its side doorways and the beacon at its end. It is now in a single
  constrained hue, ember amber, with a line of sodium ceiling lights.
  - The song has returned to the intro's texture, and the film returns to the intro's space.
  - The open daylight we saw is gone.
- **The walk:** the camera walks faster now, at the new tempo. The ceiling lights pass overhead on every other
  beat, so the pulse is in the space.
- **The dolly zoom:** the beacon at the end **never gets closer**. As the camera walks, the field of view widens
  so the far doorway holds its size while the corridor stretches (Vertigo; the tension of effort without arrival).
- **The first pass:** at the end of the phrase the camera passes through a side doorway and is **at the start of
  the same corridor**. The sun patch has moved; nothing else has.
- **Compression:** each pass the corridor is a little narrower and a little lower. There is no bass, so there is
  **no breathing**, and the space is airless.
- **Bar 83 beat 3:** the pulse stops and the camera stops with it.

**Camera:** 1.8-2.2 m/s (the fastest walking in the film), rigidly on axis, the gaze fixed on the beacon, and the
bob stepping in eighths. **Architecture:** compression each pass, with no breathing. **Colour:** K8, a one-hue
gamut (rule 8, Blade Runner 2049's Las Vegas). **Coupling:** 0.8. The loop is mechanical, and the music drives it
hard.

**Why:** the addendum asks for **psychological repetition**, "a visual loop that almost breaks", "Is this all
there is?" rather than eight effects for eight repetitions. The loop is one corridor, walked again and again with
one small change per pass. The palette narrows to one hue because the question narrows the world. The warmth of
"feel it grow" has turned into pressure, and the lyric of verse 2 already said "as the fires keep burning".

### 10. "Is that all?", bars 84-91 (182.43-199.73 s): *the loop almost breaks*

**Music:** **the beat returns** at bar 84 with the sub and the strongest offbeat hats in the song. "Is that all? /
is that all you got? / is that all I show you?" **The fill:** the low end drops out on bar 91 beats 3-4
(198.65-199.73 s).

**What we see.**
- **Bar 84:** with the beat back, the corridor turns into **the square stairwell**: four flights round a void,
  each a stair up (the Penrose loop).
  - The camera climbs one flight, turns, climbs the next, and after four flights arrives at the **same landing**,
    one storey higher. Across the void it sees the flights it has already climbed.
  - The question is made spatial: effort without arrival.
- **The breathing returns** with the sub, and **the walls begin to crack**. Thin seams open between the wall
  planes, and **slivers of sky-blue daylight leak in**. Each time round they are a little wider, then close again:
  false arrivals (Butler), the palette almost opening. The motes glint on the offbeat hats.
- **Bar 90:** on the last "is that all?" the camera arrives at the top landing, and the beacon is **right there**:
  a plain doorway three metres ahead, full of warm light.
- **The fill (198.65 s):** on bar 91 beat 3 the camera takes the last steps. For two beats the doorway's light
  fills the frame, a bright threshold.

**Camera:** 1.6-2.0 m/s climbing, circling, the gaze lifting as it climbs. **Architecture:** the Penrose screw,
breathing, and seams opening and closing (continuous offsets of the wall planes). **Colour:** K8 → K9. The
amber lightens and the blue leaks grow. On the fill the amber deepens for two beats: the last held breath.
**Coupling:** 0.9, the tightest in the film.

**Why:** the owner's "return to four-on-the-floor… treat the fill as a transitional visual event. The world should
rapidly reconfigure or reveal a new spatial destination rather than simply flash." The fill is the camera crossing
the threshold it has been walking towards since the first shot of the film.

### 11. Final chorus, "let it go", bars 92-99 (199.73-217.03 s): *the exit from the labyrinth*

**Music:** the release. The F world at full size, loud but **sustained**, not punched: the low end held and the
harmony descending to F every four bars. "Let it go", repeated.

**What we see.**
- **Bar 92 downbeat:** the camera comes out of the light, and **the building opens**.
  - It is standing on a landing in the open air. The ceiling is gone and there is sky.
  - The walls of the stairwell it came through are **separating**, drifting slowly apart in the daylight like the
    petals of something opening.
  - The palette opens to the whole wheel for the first time: sky blue, white sun, the rose and ultramarine planes
    from the dance section returning, and the plaster lit warm.
- **The climb:** a long stair goes up from the landing into the open. It began inside and ends in the air
  (Belvedere). **The camera climbs it with a clear direction**, steadily, on axis, and the fog is pushed far out to
  the horizon.
- **The second "let it go":** the breathing is back, **bigger and slower** than ever, like deep breaths after
  crying.

**Camera:** 1.4-1.6 m/s, climbing, steady, symmetric, the gaze ahead and a little up. **Architecture:** separation
and unfolding (continuous), and slow deep breathing. **Colour:** K9 → K10 over the doorway crossing (one bar).
**Coupling:** 0.5. The world is no longer pushed around; it breathes on its own.

**Why:** the addendum asks for "release… the wandering has led somewhere", and for the final "let it go" to feel
different from the first: "release / acceptance / movement forward" rather than "surrender / pause / uncertainty".
The first "let it go" stopped the camera at the top of a stair and sent it **down** into grey fog. The second sends
it **up** a stair into daylight. The same words and the same motif, in the opposite direction.

### 12. Call and response, bars 100-113 (217.03-247.30 s): *"steps in a process / for your life"*

**Music:** the call and response ("just steps in a process / for your life / (make you feel right)"), thickening;
**bar 108 (234.33 s) is the last lift, and bars 108-113 are the loudest in the song**.

**What we see.**
- **Bars 100-107:** the camera walks up through **the building's parts standing free in the open**: doorways,
  landings, flights of stairs and wall planes, spaced out across a bright landscape of fog and sky. They are the
  labyrinth's pieces, no longer joined into rooms.
  - Every doorway is lit.
  - The figure stands at the top of the highest stair, silhouetted against the sky, and the camera climbs towards
    it.
  - Each "for your life" is phrase-long, and the camera passes one doorway per call: "steps in a process" made
    literal, calmly.
- **Bar 108:** the camera arrives beside the figure and turns to look where it looks. **Below them lies the whole
  building:**
  - the first corridor;
  - the hall;
  - the enfilade;
  - the Penrose stairwell;
  - the growing room.
  - Every space of the film is laid out and **coherent**, its impossible connections visible at last as one
    beautiful object.
- **The alignment:** the scattered fragments drift into alignment, so from this one point they read as **a single
  doorway framing the sun** (Whitney's differential alignment; the single-viewpoint illusion of OK Go's video). It
  is the film's one optical trick, kept for the moment the labyrinth makes sense.

**Camera:** 1.2 m/s climbing, arriving and turning at bar 108, then almost still, rising slowly. **Architecture:**
the fragments settle, then align. **Colour:** K10 → K11. The brightest value in the film is at bar 108, and chroma
is moderate: clarity, not spectacle. **Coupling:** 0.5 → 0.4.

**Why:** the culmination "should feel like it has progressed somewhere rather than merely becoming more intense".
The labyrinth seen whole from above is Superliminal's resolution: the confusion resolves when seen from the right
place. The figure, lost at the start of verse 1, is found. The song's loudest bars are the film's calmest
understanding. That is the contrast the owner asked for.

### 13. Outro, bars 114-116 (247.30-253.79 s): *acceptance*

**Music:** the sustain rings on, the bass leaves at bar 115, and a near-silent tail follows.

**What we see.** The camera and the figure stand looking out. The air brightens into even white-gold haze
(Turrell), and the building below dissolves into it. Chroma falls, and value rises to near white. The last image is
light, not black, and it holds still through the tail.

**Camera:** easing to 0. **Architecture:** still, dissolving into haze. **Colour:** K11 → K12. **Coupling:** 0.

**Why:** "The world does not necessarily need to become spectacular. It might simply become clearer, brighter, more
spacious, more coherent, less fragmented." It ends in the white of relief (Jonauskaite: white goes with relief).

## 8. The sound-to-behaviour vocabulary

Every mapping follows the owner's chain: audio, then musical feature, then smoothed signal or event, then visual
behaviour, then a continuously evolving parameter (rule 9). **Each layer has one visual family, and nothing reacts to
everything.** The coupling column in §7 scales them all.

| musical feature | smoothing | behaviour | where it is on | where it is off, and why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sub and bass** (20-150 Hz energy) | attack about 0.3 s, release about 2 s, then a spring (ζ about 0.6, about 0.4 Hz) | **The world breathes**: a very low-frequency warp and ±1.5 % room scale. Bass is large, heavy and slow. | dance, verses, bridge, "feel it grow" (lightly), bars 84-91, and the release (largest and slowest) | bar 42 and the break (silence); "is that all you" (no sub, an airless loop); the outro |
| **Beat and tempo** (the grid, not onsets) | none; the grid is exact | **The walker's step**: a centimetre of bob and a small pace surge on each step | verses, the stripped "let it go" groove, the loop (in eighths) | holds; the intro; the release (the walk becomes a climb, smooth) |
| **The eighth-note pulse** (bars 76-83) | none | **Architecture passing**: sodium lights spaced so they pass overhead with the pulse at the camera's speed | the loop | everywhere else |
| **Hats and high percussion** (7-16 kHz onsets) | attack 50 ms, release 0.6 s | **Motes glint** in the light shafts; a faint shimmer on the fogged edge lines. Highs are small, fine and bright. | dance, "feel it grow" (motes rise), "is that all?" (offbeat glints) | "let it go", the break, "is that all you" (no hats in the music) |
| **Noise sweeps at phrase ends** (spectral flatness peaks) | a one-bar envelope | **Nearby surfaces tremble**: tiny, high-frequency, only within a few metres. Roughness is local and jagged. | about eight phrase ends (bars 29, 33, 41, 54, 58, 62, 66, 99) | everywhere else. It is rare on purpose. |
| **The voice** (centre-panned harmonic energy, 250 Hz-4 kHz) | attack 0.2 s, release 1.5 s | **The beacon warms and brightens a little; the gaze eases towards it.** The voice steers attention (Chion). | wherever the voice sings, most of all "let it go" and "feel it grow" | the break; instrumental passages (the gaze wanders) |
| **Harmony** (the bar's chord) | one-bar crossfade | **Fog hue drifts a few degrees** (Cm cool, F warm): the fast colour voice | throughout, at a very small depth | none |
| **Section** (the score) | the timeline, 4-8 bar curves | **Palette position, coupling, camera speed, architecture state**: the slow voices | throughout | — |
| **Silence** | — | **Real absence**: the breathing stops, the motes hang, the camera holds | bar 1; bar 17 beat 4 (one beat); bar 42; bars 74-75; bar 91 beats 3-4; the tail | — |

**What the music never does:** move the camera by a hit, flash anything on a beat, change colour on a beat, shake
anything, or switch any geometry. Every musical influence goes through a smooth second-order response (the
engineer's `spring`) or changes a rate (`integrate`).

threshold at a phrase boundary, and each swap is a moment the music also turns.

## 10. The climax and why it is earned

The release at bar 92 is earned by four things set up earlier and withheld until then:

1. **Daylight** has been withheld for 200 s, glimpsed once through a doorway at "feel it grow", and then taken away
   by the loop.
2. **The beacon** has been visible and unreachable since the first shot. At bar 92 we pass through it.
3. **The palette** has been narrowed to one hue for 34 s just before. Opening to the full wheel is the largest
   colour change the film can make, and it is made once.
4. **The stair** has taken us down into grey fog at the first "let it go". At the second, it takes us up into the
   sky.

The film's peak of **energy** (the loop, bars 76-91) and its peak of **release** (bars 92-113) are deliberately
different: tight, mechanical and airless, then open, slow-breathing and clear. The loudest bars of the song (108-113)
are the film's moment of understanding, not its densest. That is the addendum's "contrast is more important than
constant intensity".

## 11. Shot tests, and the failures to watch for

Before approving any stretch, ask the owner's question: *does this make the viewer feel something, or merely
demonstrate something the renderer can do?* Then check it against these:

- **Loneliness:** is the frame mostly empty? Is there one idea in it?
- **Wandering:** is the camera moving, or deliberately holding? Does it ever look like a drone or a ride?
- **Repetition:** can the viewer recognise a place or a move from earlier? Is exactly one thing different?
- **Restraint:** is anything reacting that the music does not justify? Is anything reacting on every beat?
- **Colour:** has daylight or clear sky leaked before bar 67? Is any frame black-dominant? Does the palette move
  faster than the phrase?
- **Tone:** does anything read as horror (a figure too close, a figure facing us, darkness for its own sake, a
  chase)?
- **Technical:**
  - camera clearance;
  - swaps visible outside their thresholds;
  - edge-line shimmer at distance;
  - volumetric speckle in the shafts;
  - seams at the screw wrap.

## 14. Risks and fallbacks

| risk | fallback |
|---|---|
| **Chapters** are not ready | Run the whole film in two cells: the corridor (A) and a combined stairwell and hall (B + D). Morph the corridor into the enfilade. Let the release open cell A itself (the walls separate and the ceiling lifts). The loop still returns to the first corridor, and the arc survives. |
| **One material per world** | Colour planes become light instead of paint: the rose comes from coloured light falling on the plaster. The palette still works, with less graphic power. |
| **The figure** reads wrong | Drop it. Scale comes from door heights, risers and rails. The bar-108 arrival becomes the camera alone at the top, looking down on the whole building. |
| **Performance** in the open (long rays and many fragments) | Fog out far fragments sooner, and bring `maxDistance` in to the fog's reach. The release reads through light and sky more than geometry. |
| The **room-growth** or **separation** transforms clip the camera | Author the path along the transform's invariant axis (the room grows away from the path), and rely on the collision guard as a safety net. |
