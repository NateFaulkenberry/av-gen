# Glowmere: the soundtrack, and what answers it

How `examples/world/glowmere-stylized.json` is scored, and the reasoning behind every modulation
route in it. The scene itself is described in [stylized-glowmere.md](stylized-glowmere.md) and its
look is audited in [glowmere-world-builder-audit.md](glowmere-world-builder-audit.md). Nothing in
the composition, the light rig or the camera timeline was changed to write this; the whole of the
audio response lives in the project file's `assets` and `routes` blocks.

## The track

```json
"assets": {
 "scene": { "kind": "composition", "path": "glowmere-stylized.scene.json" },
 "audio": { "path": "../../assets/audio/glowmere-valley.wav" }
}
```

`assets.audio.path` is the key the project loader reads (`Engine::applyProject`, resolved relative
to the project file); the same shape is used by
[constellation.json](../examples/constellation/constellation.json). The file is 90 seconds of
D Dorian at 72 bpm, generated by `tools/make_glowmere_score.py`, and is **not committed** — the WAV
is 17 MB, deterministic, and gitignored. Regenerate it before rendering:

```sh
python3 tools/make_glowmere_score.py assets/audio/glowmere-valley.wav --seconds 90
```

A missing file is a warning, not an error: the project still loads, the audio-sourced routes sit
unresolved, and the scene renders silent and static. `assets/audio/manifest.json` carries the
expected SHA-256.

The arrangement matters to what follows. It is monotonic for the first half — pad in at 10%, pulse
at 22%, bell and air ramping — and then, deliberately, it empties out. Everything but the air bed
hushes from 55% to 60% of the piece (**49.5 s to 54.0 s**) and returns, slightly louder than it
left, over the following bar and a half (settling by **59.4 s**). That is the break and the drop,
and it exists because a score that only ever rises can never produce one.

## The three tiers

### Tier 1 — continuous (unchanged)

Two routes, both pre-existing, both smoothed over seconds. They are the texture layer: no event in
them, just the track's weight leaning on the scene.

| Source | Target | Op | Amount | Attack / decay | Rest value |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---:|
| `audio.bass` | `material/paintedCrown/emissionIntensity` | add | +0.16 | 1200 / 3000 ms | 1.7 |
| `audio.treble` | `particles/spores/spawnRate` | add | +24 | 600 / 2000 ms | 300 |

### Tier 2 — the pulse

| Source | Target | Op | Amount | Attack / decay | Rest value |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---:|
| `music.beat` | `material/glowmereTissue/emissionIntensity` | add | +0.09 | 25 / 240 ms | 2.0 |
| `music.impact` | `material/glowmereTissue/emissionIntensity` | add | +0.22 | 12 / 900 ms | 2.0 |
| `music.downbeat` | `nodes/elder-filaments/emissiveBoost` | add | +0.10 | 45 / 620 ms | 1.0 |
| `music.downbeat` | `lightrig/GlowmereValley/elder-practical/intensity` | add | +0.30 | 60 / 900 ms | 3.0 |

**Why `glowmereTissue` and nothing else carries the beat.** That material program is referenced by
exactly three of the thirteen scatter layers: `fungi` (emission 6.888), `shelf-fungi` (4.428) and
`beacons` (6.15) — the top of the audit's luminance ladder, and the three layers that are both the
brightest and the smallest and rarest. Ten layers, including every layer that fills the frame, do
not reference it and cannot see this route at all. So the beat moves the 1,771 instances that are
already reading as points of light, and leaves the other 111,789 — every tree, fern, grass blade,
bush, flower, boulder and pebble in the valley — completely still. That is the audit's §1.1
hierarchy used as a modulation target rather than fought against: the ladder's top rungs twinkle,
its bottom rungs do not.

At 72 bpm a beat lands every 833 ms; a 240 ms decay returns the tissue to rest well before the next
one, so it reads as a pulse rather than a raised floor. `music.impact` lands on the same parameter
with 2.4× the amount and a 900 ms decay, so one target gives two distinct gestures — a twinkle on
the beat, a flare on a hit — without adding a second reactive subject to the frame.

**Why the downbeat is the hero's, twice.** The elder owns the only warm light in the world
(audit §1.2), so it is the thing the eye is already on and the thing that should most clearly
answer. `nodes/elder-filaments/emissiveBoost` is a per-node multiplier on the 38 warm threads
(`emissiveIntensity = rest × boost`), so +0.10 is a 10% lift on the one warm accent and touches
nothing else in the scene. The paired route on the elder's green practical makes it a *light* event
as well as a surface one: the lamp under the crown reaches 10% further across the ground below it,
so the downbeat is something the world is lit by, not just something that glows.

### Tier 3 — form

| Source | Target | Op | Amount | Attack / decay | Rest value |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---:|
| `music.phrase` | `particles/spores/turbulence` | add | +0.18 | 1400 / 3600 ms | 0.8 |
| `music.section` | `nodes/elder-crown/emissiveBoost` | add | +0.06 | 2200 / 6000 ms | 1.0 |
| `music.build` | `scene/windSpeed` | add | +0.18 | 2400 / 2800 ms | 0.74 |
| `music.build` | `particles/spores/spawnRate` | add | +70 | 2600 / 2400 ms | 300 |
| `music.break` | `scene/windSpeed` | add | **−0.20** | 1500 / 3000 ms | 0.74 |
| `music.break` | `particles/spores/spawnRate` | add | **−80** | 1400 / 3000 ms | 300 |
| `music.drop` | `nodes/elder-filaments/emissiveBoost` | add | +0.18 | 70 / 2200 ms | 1.0 |
| `music.drop` | `procedural/elder-filaments/distribution/radius` | add | +0.30 | 320 / 2600 ms | 6.8 |
| `music.drop` | `particles/spores/burst` | add | +220 | 10 / 260 ms | 0 |
| `music.drop` | `scene/volumeScattering` | add | +0.09 | 250 / 1800 ms | 0.5 |

**The build is motion, not brightness.** Raising `scene/windSpeed` by 24% makes every plant in the
valley lean and gust harder — thirteen scatter layers, the foreground leaves and the elder's
filaments all move more — and it costs the luminance ladder nothing, because nothing gets brighter.
Anticipation is the one thing a scene can express without spending any of its contrast budget, and
wind is how this scene already expresses it. The paired spore route thickens the air in the same
breath: more motes gathering, still drifting at the same speed.

**The break is the only place anything goes down, and both routes are negative on purpose.** The
brief for a break is that the world breathes, so the wind falls by 27% and the spore spawn rate by
80/s. `scene/windSpeed` clamps at 0 and `particles/spores/spawnRate` at 0, so a strong break cannot
drive either negative. Build and break never overlap in this arrangement, so the two pairs never
cancel; if they did, the sum is still bounded and still sane.

**The drop is the only event the environment itself answers.** Four routes, each in a different
system, and only one of them is an emission:

- the hero's warm threads surge 18% and settle over 2.2 seconds — with a downbeat's +0.10 landing
  inside the same moment, the worst case is a 28% lift on the one warm accent, at the single
  largest moment in the piece;
- `procedural/elder-filaments/distribution/radius` widens the ring of 38 hanging threads from 6.8 m
  to 7.1 m and back. This is a 4.4% change to an *instance distribution*, not to a mesh: it
  regenerates a 38-point cloud, re-uploads 38 instance transforms, and leaves the shared tube
  geometry alone. Together with the emissive surge it reads as the elder opening;
- a 220-particle spore burst, which over the route's 260 ms decay releases roughly 900 extra motes
  against a steady-state population near 6,600 and a capacity of 10,240;
- `scene/volumeScattering` rises 18%, so the air itself carries more light for a second and a half.
  This is the "major environmental response" done the way the audit says this scene works: through
  atmosphere, which §1.6 already tunes to the valley's size, rather than by turning anything up.

## What is deliberately left still

The most important rule in the audit is that contrast carries the look, and a scene where
everything pulses has no pulse. The following were considered as targets and rejected:

- **Ten of the thirteen scatter layers.** `canopy`, `pines`, `deadwood`, `bushes`, `ferns`,
  `grass`, `fan-plants`, `flowers`, `boulders` and `pebbles` have no audio response whatsoever.
  They hold still so that the three that move are legible as moving. `flowers` is the deliberate
  edge case: at 3.936 it is the fourth-brightest layer in the world and it still does not react,
  because a scene where *every* bright thing pulses has flattened the ladder it was given.
- **`nodes/valley/emissiveBoost`.** One route here would lift all thirteen layers at once. It is
  the single most tempting target in the parameter set and it destroys the entire hierarchy — the
  0.035 trees and the 6.888 fungi would rise together, which is precisely the "brighten everything"
  failure the audit warns about.
- **`post/bloom/intensity`.** Bloom at 0.18 against a threshold of 1.0 is the reason the bright
  fungi glow instead of smearing (audit §1.7); only the ladder's top two rungs cross the threshold
  at all. Modulating it would change *which* things bloom, not just how much, which is a change to
  the look rather than a response to the music.
- **The grade and the exposure.** `post/grade/*`, `camera/exposure/*` and the −1.3 EV compensation
  are the shot's decisions, not the music's. A hue shift on a section change was tried on paper and
  dropped: it is the sort of thing that reads as a video effect rather than as a place.
- **The moon.** `lightrig/GlowmereValley/moon/*` and the ambient are untouched. The rig's whole
  premise (§1.5) is that the landscape's form comes from a 22° moon raking across it; a key light
  that answers the beat is a nightclub, not a valley.
- **`elder-stem` and `foreground-leaves`.** The stem stays at its 0.1 emission and the foreground
  plant at 0.04. The hero reads because its three parts behave *differently* — a cap that barely
  moves, threads that do (§1.3) — and making all three answer the music would erase that.
- **The camera.** The 90-second timeline is a shot, not a template. No route targets
  `camera/position`, `camera/target` or the lens.

Four of the eleven contract signals are also unused, on purpose:

- **`music.bar`** sits between `music.beat` and `music.phrase`, both of which are already spoken
  for. A third periodic modulator on the same subjects turns three distinct rhythms into one
  average.
- **`music.energyRise` / `music.energyDrop`** are level-shaped restatements of `music.build` and
  `music.break`. Routing both pairs to the same targets would double the amounts at exactly the
  moments the build and break already fire, and would smear the edges of both.
- **`music.beat` on the elder.** The hero answers the downbeat and the drop, never the plain beat.
  A living thing that opens and glows is not a strobe, and at 72 bpm a per-beat flare on the only
  warm light in the frame would be the first thing anyone noticed and the only thing they
  remembered.

## Amount discipline

Every event route is between 4% and 24% of its target's rest value. The largest single amount is
`music.build` on `scene/windSpeed` at 24%, and it is the one route that changes no brightness at
all. The largest brightness amount is `music.impact` on `glowmereTissue` at 11%. The largest
compounded case is the elder's filaments during a drop that contains a downbeat: +0.28 on a boost
of 1.0, which is 28%, once, at the piece's largest moment.

Measured against a variant of the same project with the fourteen event routes removed, at
640×360:

| Window | Pixels differing | Peak per-pixel difference | Mean frame difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 18–24 s (beats, no structural event) | 0.15 – 0.59% | up to 115 / 255 | 0.004 – 0.029 |
| 48–60 s (break, then the drop) | 0.08 – 0.27% | up to 136 / 255 | 0.001 – 0.040 |

That shape is the goal, not an accident: a fraction of a percent of the frame changes, and where it
changes it changes a lot. The music moves the small bright things hard and moves the frame's mean
by a fortieth of a code value. Note that the difference grows monotonically across each window
because the spore spawn-rate routes make the two particle populations diverge and 14–30 s lifetimes
keep them diverged; that is particle history, not creeping brightness.

## Verification, and what is not verified

Verified by running the engine:

```sh
./build/release/src/avgen --headless --project examples/world/glowmere-stylized.json \
  --render /tmp/glowaudio --format png --range 8:8.05 --fps 30 --size 640x360
```

- The project loads with **854 parameters, 16 routes, 0 warnings**, and the render completes with
  **zero GPU errors**. Zero warnings is load-bearing: every one of the sixteen route targets
  resolved to a real, modulatable parameter, and the audio file was found. Pointing
  `assets.audio.path` at a missing file produces `project: audio: audio file not found: …`, so the
  clean load is evidence and not silence.
- `offline analysis: 8434 frames` — the 90-second track is read and analysed ahead of the render,
  which is what makes an offline render and a live window agree about where the events are.
- The eleven `music.<name>` signals are declared by `src/app/music_runtime.hpp` and all fourteen
  event routes bind. The names in the enum table (`beat`, `downbeat`, `bar`, `phrase`, `section`,
  `energyRise`, `energyDrop`, `build`, `break`, `drop`, `impact`) match the route sources exactly.
- The events fire on this track: a full 0–90 s render with every event amount multiplied by 25
  differs from the no-events variant on 899 of 900 frames.
- No measurable cost. A 30-frame 640×360 render with all sixteen routes bound and firing every
  frame ran at 11.2 fps against 10.8 fps for the two-route variant, and a full 90-second pass at
  both settings reported the same frame timings and no particle-capacity warnings. The per-frame
  regeneration of the filament instance cloud is 38 points and does not show up.

Not verified:

- **Nobody has watched this at speed with sound.** Every number above is a still-frame or hash
  comparison. Whether a 240 ms twinkle on the fungi reads as musical or as noise, and whether the
  drop lands with the camera, is an audition question and has not been auditioned.
- **The detector's event times on this specific track were not enumerated.** It is established that
  events fire across the whole 90 seconds and that the break and drop are written into the score at
  49.5 s and 54.0 s, but no per-event timestamp log was produced, so the claim "the drop route
  fires at the drop" rests on the score's construction and the classifier's tests rather than on a
  direct observation. A 0.2 s window sampled at 54.6 s showed no difference between the scored and
  unscored variants, which is consistent with the drop firing outside that particular window but is
  not evidence that it fires correctly.
- **Interaction with the other work in flight.** `music.*` was landed by concurrent work and the
  scene file is being edited concurrently. The route targets `nodes/elder-filaments/*`,
  `nodes/elder-crown/*`, `procedural/elder-filaments/distribution/radius` and
  `lightrig/GlowmereValley/elder-practical/intensity` are all name-derived: renaming a node, the
  light, or the rig unbinds those routes with a warning rather than an error.
