# ADR-2229: the Crowd Navigation Lab, and the numbers it holds the crowd to

- **Status:** Accepted (2026-11-26), on `eng/115-crowds` (115 programme, Agent 3). Environments e01, e02, e03 and e08 exist; e04–e07 come with phase B3 and the navmesh.
- **Code:**
  - `examples/labs/crowd/` (`build.py`, `cases.json`, `eNN-*.json`);
  - `LabId::Crowd` in `src/labs/lab.{hpp,cpp}`;
  - the overlay profile in `src/labs/overlays.cpp`;
  - `docs/crowd-lab/README.md`.
- **Tests:** `tests/unit/test_crowd_world.cpp`; `tests/unit/test_lab_registry.cpp` (16 labs).

## Decision

1. **The lab follows Agent 4's conventions** (115 plan §8):
   - one project per environment;
   - generated by a stdlib `build.py` whose `--check` is a CPU test;
   - case numbers are 100 × environment + k;
   - only procedural content and the tracked CC0 alien.
2. **Every case's expectation is a CPU assertion**, not a picture:
   - no two agents overlap by more than 0.03 m at any step;
   - no wall penetration above 0.01 m;
   - every agent arrives;
   - path queries per step never exceed `pathsPerStep`;
   - a walking body's planted toe slips at most 15% of its speed (median);
   - a checkpointed seek equals a from-zero replay at ten seeded random seconds;
   - 1, 4 and 8 threads give one state hash;
   - a 30 fps play equals a cold seek;
   - one seek lands entities and crowd where a play does, at times inside a departure stagger, a walk and a dwell.
3. **The lab's overlay is the crowd debug layer** (walls, corridors, goals, agents by state). It is an instrument: it does not draw into another lab's measured frame.

## Consequences

The bottleneck environment's destination area was enlarged until 200 arrivals fit at about 1.4 m spacing. A destination packed tighter than a body can walk between makes the last arrivals wedge, and the crowd now calls an agent wedged within 2.4 m of its slot arrived. Authors must size destination areas for their crowd; the lab README says so.
