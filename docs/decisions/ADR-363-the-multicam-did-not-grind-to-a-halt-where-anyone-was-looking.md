# ADR-363: The multicam did not grind to a halt where anybody was looking

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-09-19
**Follows:** ADR-170 (one GPU, one run), ADR-182 (a probe that cannot fail), ADR-267 / ADR-273 (what
a seek costs and the unit it is paid in), ADR-300 (the pose layer stack), ADR-344 (the showcase came
home), ADR-355 (five frames a second was a bounding box)

> *"`examples/world/glowmere-valley-2-multicam.json` performance has ground to a halt since the
> project was modernized onto the new animation system."*

Three instruments were pointed at that sentence. Two of them say the modernisation cost about
twelve per cent. The third says the film has a 2.7-second stall in it, that the stall is the thing
anybody would call grinding to a halt, and that **the modernisation made it 27% faster**.

---

## 1. What was measured, and with what

One engine binary per table. `tools/gpu-lock.sh` around every run that touches the GPU. Arms are
*data*: nine copies of `glowmere-valley-2-multicam`, generated by `tools/make_mcperf_arms.py` from
the suspect commits and from HEAD with one thing put back, so a difference between two of them
cannot be a compiler flag, a shader edit or a stale `src/avgen`. Interleaved round-robin, three
rounds, minima over rounds, with the paired per-round differences beside them because three other
agents were building and rendering throughout.

## 2. The offline render: +12.6%, and all of it is the trees

`avgen --headless --project <arm> --frames 900 --size 1920x1080`, 900 frames of the film's own
timeline from t = 0.

| arm | what it is | wall median (min over 3) | gpu median (min over 3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `base` | `ca57a3a4~1`, before any of it | **27.57 ms** | 21.17 ms |
| `cast194` | `ca57a3a4` the cast at 1.94× | 27.69 | 20.97 |
| `wide50` | `4c8a1323` the 50%/20% dolly | 27.70 | 21.04 |
| `trees` | `570e41c2` valley 3's trees and the `decide` cast | 30.12 | 23.07 |
| `rebake` | `9433044d` the cut re-baked | 32.05 | 25.30 |
| `farm` | `3dff0d28` the farm animals' ground settings | 31.91 | 25.23 |
| `head` | `1706a5e5` | **31.05 ms** | 23.79 |

36 fps to 32 fps. A regression, and not a halt.

The step is at `570e41c2` and nowhere else, and one isolation arm says what in it: `oldtrees` is
HEAD in every respect but the terrain's scatter table, and it comes back to **28.37 ms / 21.76 ms**,
within 0.6 ms of `base`, while keeping the ported `decide` cast, the pose layers, the re-baked cut
and the farm-animal settings. 638 tree instances became 2,872 (ADR-344 §2). Paired per round, the
tree table costs +3.67, +3.73, +2.03 ms of GPU.

**Neither the cast, the animation layers nor the 1.94× scale costs anything measurable.**

## 3. The editor: 8.9 ms, and it is not where the complaint lives

`avgen --project <arm> --ui-script idle --play --frames 600 --size 2560x1440 --profile-cpu`.

| arm | FRAME min | FRAME median | p95 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `head` | 6.657 ms | **8.920 ms** | 18.258 ms |
| `base` | 5.754 | 8.418 | 17.256 |

113 fps against 119. Half a millisecond, in the same direction as §2 and about the same size.

## 4. The scrub: 2.75 seconds a click, and it predates the port

`--ui-script scrub`, same binary, same window, same 180 frames. The arm drags the playhead; the
profile's own `engine.seek` row is what one drag frame costs.

| arm | FRAME median | `entity re-sim` median | body-steps replayed |
|---|---|---|---|
| `head` | **2,755 ms** | 2,747.8 ms | 118,801 |
| `base` | **3,768 ms** | 3,760.7 ms | 118,801 |

Both replay exactly the same 118,801 body-steps — the figure ADR-273 records for this scene — so
the difference is entirely the cost of one body-step, and `decide` is **27% cheaper per step than
the `explore` it replaced**. The port did not cause this. The port improved it.

This is the number that matches the owner's sentence. A 2.7-second stall on every timeline click is
what "ground to a halt" describes; a 31 ms frame is not. It was 3.8 seconds before the work the
complaint is attached to, which is exactly why the complaint arrived attached to the wrong change:
the film became worth scrubbing when its characters started moving, and the stall had been sitting
there the whole time.

**The budget is not the lever.** `SeekBudget::maxBodySteps` is 180,000 in the live editor; this
scene costs 118,801 and is therefore never clamped. Lowering it does not make the seek cheaper, it
makes it land somewhere else — ADR-273 measured a 45 s window putting a body 195.4 m from where the
full replay puts it. A faster seek that draws a different frame is not a faster seek.

**Where the 23 µs per body-step goes is not established here**, and the next person should not guess
at it. One lead is recorded rather than acted on: this film's nav grid is **refused at load** —
*"the grid will NOT answer for this world's terrain — it disagreed with the world after 261 sampled
walk(s); every walkability query stays analytic"* — so every walkability and clearance query in the
replay samples the world function instead of reading a cell. That is a measurement away from being
either the answer or eliminated, and `tools/seek_probe.cpp` is the instrument ADR-273 built for it.

## 5. The supervisor's hypothesis, and the arm that came out the other way

The application was observed writing this project back to disk without `cameraAimFollow` (37
entries) or `cameraShotSpans` (42). The hypothesis was that the camera was then being re-derived
live, at a cost.

Both halves are wrong, and the second is wrong by measurement.

**It is not the serializer.** `avgen --headless --project ... --save-project ...` round-trips both
keys intact, and the headless save and the application's save differ in nothing else: both write the
same 5,530 parameters, the same 28 additions over the file on disk, and no losses. What the
application's save lost is exactly the six targets `directedCameraTargets()` owns plus the two baked
tables — the precise signature of `releaseDirectedCamera`, which is what a viewport drag calls once
`refreshDirection` has taken up the claim on a project that arrives already directed. The save was
faithful; the cut had already been deleted, on purpose, by a documented path.

**It is not a cost either.** `nobake` is HEAD with all eight removed, and it is **1.6 ms faster**
(29.44 ms / 22.54 ms against 31.05 / 23.79), because a cut that pushes in on its subjects puts more
foliage in frame than the wide default does. The arm could have come out the other way and did
(ADR-182).

That leaves a real defect with no performance story attached: reaching for the viewport destroys a
42-shot bake that took a re-cut to produce, and the next Save writes the loss to disk. It is
out of scope here and is written down in §7.

## 6. Decision

**Nothing is changed for performance.** Three levers were measured and all three were refused:

* **The tree table.** It is what the owner asked for and it is 3.7 ms.
* **`treelod`** — the distance gates the port flattened, put back. `570e41c2` copied valley 3's
  eight-layer table in wholesale, and `deadwood` arrived carrying viewDistance 520 /
  minScreenRadius 1.0 over the 380 / 1.6 it had been authored with; the four secondary species at
  5.4–7.1 m carry the same relaxed gates. Restoring them is measured at **−0.92, −0.98, −4.26 ms**
  of GPU across three paired rounds, median −0.98 ms, about 4%. It costs distant silhouette on five
  of eight species for four per cent, and it is kept generable by `make_mcperf_arms.py` rather than
  applied.
* **The seek budget.** §4.

The animation defect ADR-344 left behind is fixed, and that is a separate record: the attend `Pose`
action now carries its considerer's subject, so all five aliens turn their heads instead of one.

## Rejected alternatives

* **Cut the tree densities back.** `canopy`'s own forest density went 0.0045 → 0.0072 in the port
  without ADR-344 arguing for it, and reverting it would buy instances back. Refused without
  measuring it, because the owner's sentence about this film was *"the hills carry a canopy"* and
  the frame that proves it is ADR-344 §5's `cairn-cap` pair. Thinning the thing somebody asked for,
  to recover part of four per cent, is not a fix.
* **Shorten the live seek window.** ADR-273 already priced the frame this loses. Rejected there,
  rejected again here.
* **Fix the nav grid's disagreement with the terrain so walkability stops being analytic.** It is
  the best lead in §4 and it is not a lead anybody has costed. Guessing at it inside a performance
  brief is how the last three wrong conclusions in this repository were reached.
* **Report the 12.6% as the answer.** It is a true number about the wrong instrument. The owner is
  not watching a headless render; they are dragging a playhead that takes two and three quarter
  seconds to answer.

## Revisit triggers

* **A scrub costs 2.75 s and nobody knows why.** 118,801 body-steps at 23 µs each. `seek_probe`'s
  `kind` arm, pointed at this population, is one run away from attributing it.
* **The nav grid refuses this terrain.** 261 sampled walks disagreed. Every Glowmere scene shares
  this terrain, so whatever this costs, it costs five times.
* **A viewport drag deletes the cut.** `releaseDirectedCamera` erases `cameraAimFollow`,
  `cameraShotSpans` and six timeline tracks; the next Save writes the deletion to disk, and the
  re-bake that puts them back re-photographs the hero anchors (ADR-344 §4) and so cannot be run
  casually. Observed on the owner's own file on 2026-09-19.
* **`explore` is 1.4× `decide` per body-step.** Measured incidentally here on one scene. If that
  holds generally it is the cheapest available answer to ADR-267's blocker and nobody has looked.
