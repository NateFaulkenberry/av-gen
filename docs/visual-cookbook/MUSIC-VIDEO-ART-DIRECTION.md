# AV Gen Music Video

## Art Direction & Visual Design Bible

**Status:** Foundational / Living Document
**Project:** AV Gen
**Primary Visual Concept:** Stylized miniature metropolitan music-video world
**Core Inspirations:** The Sims, SimCity, miniature architectural dioramas, stylized cinematic animation, New York City
**Core Visual Arc:** Gray → Color → Glow → Motion → Transformation → Surrealism

---

# 1. Vision

The music video should take place inside a stylized miniature metropolitan world that combines the approachable visual language of **The Sims** and **SimCity** with the cinematic presentation of a carefully constructed miniature diorama.

The world should initially feel ordinary, quiet, slightly artificial and predominantly gray.

The protagonist inhabits this world as another gray clay-like humanoid.

As the music begins to affect the protagonist, it begins to affect the environment.

Color appears.

Lights respond.

People begin moving rhythmically.

Architecture begins responding.

The city becomes increasingly vibrant, luminous, animated and surreal until the entire environment effectively becomes a physical manifestation of the music.

The fundamental creative concept is:

> **The music doesn't simply play over the world. The music gradually brings the world to life.**

The visual progression should therefore be treated as a narrative system rather than a collection of unrelated visual effects.

---

# 2. Core Artistic Principle

The project should avoid the appearance of:

* a generic game-engine demo
* a photorealistic city
* a random collection of asset-store models
* a conventional audio visualizer
* a collection of objects simply pulsing to the beat
* overly detailed photorealistic environments
* excessive post-processing used to disguise weak underlying art direction
* arbitrary procedural geometry
* generic "cyberpunk" aesthetics

Instead, the target is:

> **A coherent, handcrafted-feeling miniature animated world whose visual language remains recognizable from the largest aerial shot to the smallest character close-up.**

Every asset, shader, animation, camera move and musical reaction should reinforce this shared visual identity.

# 4. Overall World Style

The environment should feel like a **physical miniature that happens to be alive**.

The miniature feeling should primarily come from:

* composition
* camera perspective
* depth of field
* lighting
* simplified geometry
* material response
* object scale
* environmental density
* controlled texture detail

Do not make every object physically tiny.

The same world must support:

1. huge aerial city shots
2. neighborhood shots
3. street-level shots
4. building exterior shots
5. interior shots
6. character close-ups

The audience should be able to move seamlessly between these scales.

---

# 5. Geometry Language

Geometry should be:

* clean
* simple
* readable
* modular
* slightly exaggerated
* softly beveled
* intentionally stylized

Avoid:

* excessive micro-detail
* razor-sharp geometry
* unnecessarily dense meshes
* photogrammetry-like surfaces
* realistic architectural grime everywhere
* arbitrary geometry complexity

Objects should remain recognizable even when viewed at relatively small screen sizes.

## Bevel Philosophy

Where appropriate, objects should have subtle beveling so that:

* edges catch light
* silhouettes remain attractive
* buildings feel manufactured
* miniature objects feel tangible
* lighting creates readable gradients

The bevel should support the miniature aesthetic rather than create an overly rounded cartoon appearance.

---

# 6. Materials

Materials should generally be:

* matte
* semi-matte
* stylized
* physically plausible but art-directed

Avoid excessive glossy surfaces.

The environment should not look like polished plastic.

Material variation should primarily come from:

* roughness
* subtle color variation
* controlled normals
* simplified textures
* emissive materials
* lighting

Textures should support geometry rather than overwhelm it.

---

# 7. Color Strategy

The most important color concept is **controlled emergence of color**.

## Initial State

The world should begin relatively monochromatic.

Primary visual language:

* gray
* warm gray
* cool gray
* muted neutral architectural colors
* subdued environmental colors

The protagonist and NPCs should be particularly restrained.

## Musical Awakening

As music begins influencing the world:

### Stage 1 — Subtle Color

Small amounts of color appear through:

* windows
* signs
* streetlights
* screens
* vehicle lights
* environmental reflections

### Stage 2 — Saturation

Color becomes increasingly visible in:

* architecture
* storefronts
* lighting
* vegetation
* clothing accents
* reflections

### Stage 3 — Glow

Emissive elements begin to illuminate the world.

Examples:

* windows
* signs
* plants
* streetlights
* particles
* architectural accents
* objects associated with the protagonist

### Stage 4 — Environmental Color

The lighting environment itself becomes colorful.

Color should begin affecting:

* fog
* reflections
* shadows
* surfaces
* characters
* atmospheric particles

### Stage 5 — Transformation

Color ceases to merely decorate the environment.

It begins to **change the environment**.

# 8. Character Design

The population should use a unified stylized humanoid design.

Characters should resemble:

> **simple gray clay prototypes of Sims**

without directly reproducing proprietary Sims models.

Characteristics:

* simplified anatomy
* readable silhouettes
* slightly exaggerated head proportions
* simplified hands
* simplified facial features
* smooth gray material
* sculptural/clay-like appearance
* expressive body language

The characters should feel like they came from the same miniature world as the buildings.

---

# 9. Protagonist

The protagonist is the primary visual anchor of the music video.

He should initially be:

* male
* gray
* clay-like
* visually simple
* relatively anonymous
* recognizable primarily through silhouette and behavior

He should not initially look like a conventional heroic video-game character.

The intent is for him to feel like:

> **an ordinary inhabitant of an ordinary miniature city.**

This makes his eventual transformation much more powerful.

## Protagonist Transformation

The protagonist should gradually become affected by the music.

Potential progression:

### Beginning

Completely gray.

### Early Music

Subtle colored lighting appears around him.

### Development

Colored reflections and small particles begin following him.

### Rising Energy

His environment responds increasingly strongly to his movement.

### Climax

The protagonist can become a focal point of the world's musical transformation.

The transformation does not necessarily mean changing his entire material.

It can instead happen through:

* rim lighting
* emissive accents
* reflections
* particles
* environmental illumination
* subtle material changes
* animation
* camera treatment

---

# 10. NPC Design

NPCs should use the same gray clay design language.

At minimum, create:

* male base character
* female base character

Variation should come from modular components:

* hairstyles

* accessories
* body proportions
* poses

The project should avoid requiring hundreds of unique character models.

Instead:

> **A small number of excellent base characters + modular variation + excellent animation should create the perception of a populated city.**

# 12. Modular Building System

Buildings should not primarily be treated as unique finished meshes.

The preferred approach is modular construction.

Conceptually:

```text
BUILDING
├── BASE
├── FACADE
├── WINDOW MODULE
├── FLOOR MODULE
├── CORNER MODULE
├── ENTRANCE
├── SIGNAGE
├── BALCONY
├── FIRE ESCAPE
├── ROOFTOP
└── ROOFTOP DETAILS
```

This allows the world generator and artist to create substantial architectural variation from a relatively small number of assets.

The asset pipeline should favor:

* interchangeable components
* consistent dimensions
* predictable pivots
* consistent material conventions
* consistent scale
* reusable detail modules

# 14. Asset Acquisition Philosophy

The project should **source aggressively during the bootstrap phase**.

Do not spend months manually modeling an entire city before the visual language can be tested.

Preferred pipeline:

```text
SOURCE
  ↓
NORMALIZE
  ↓
STYLIZE
  ↓
KITBASH
  ↓
CUSTOMIZE
  ↓
HERO ASSET
```

The objective is to establish the world quickly using existing high-quality assets, then gradually replace important elements with custom artwork.

---

# 15. Asset Normalization

All externally sourced assets should pass through a common AV Gen asset standard.

The normalization pipeline should eventually support:

* scale normalization
* coordinate/orientation normalization
* origin/pivot normalization
* material normalization
* roughness normalization
* texture resolution normalization
* color-space correctness
* normal-map correctness
* LOD generation
* collision generation
* shadow configuration
* metadata
* categorization

This is critical.

The final environment must not look like a collection of unrelated marketplace assets.

The asset library should ultimately appear to belong to one unified art direction.

## Layer 3 — Cinematic

Useful:

* surprise
* fear

## Layer 4 — Musical

This is a major custom area.

Required eventually:

* head bob
* shoulder movement
* body sway
* foot tapping

* beat accents

# 18. Music-Reaction Language

The music reaction system must avoid becoming a conventional audio visualizer.

The goal is not:

> "Everything pulses on every beat."

Instead, different musical features should control different categories of visual behavior.

Potential mappings:

```text
KICK
→ physical impact
→ architecture movement
→ camera impact

BASS
→ large-scale environmental motion
→ illumination
→ structural deformation

SNARE
→ sharp visual accents
→ particles
→ light flashes
→ character gestures

HIGH FREQUENCIES
→ shimmer
→ small particles
→ fine environmental motion
→ highlights

ONSET
→ discrete events
→ transformations
→ camera cuts
→ object spawning

ENERGY
→ global color intensity
→ world saturation
→ environmental activity

TEMPO
→ animation speed
→ environmental rhythm
→ camera movement

SPECTRAL CENTROID
→ color character
→ visual sharpness
→ particle behavior
```

These mappings should remain artist-controllable.

The visual world should progress through distinct states.

## State 0 — Dormant

* normal NPC behavior

Small elements begin responding.

* lights flicker

* streetlights breathe

## State 2 — Color

Color enters the environment.

* storefronts
* windows

* clothing accents
* environmental lighting

## State 3 — Glow

Emissive elements become significant.

* glowing windows
* glowing signs
* glowing plants
* particles

* environmental illumination

## State 4 — Physical Response

The city begins to dance.

* buildings subtly deform

# 20. Camera Language

The project should support three primary camera modes.

## A. Diorama Camera

Used for:

* city establishing shots
* skyline shots
* neighborhood transitions
* large musical moments
* environmental reveals

Characteristics:

* elevated viewpoint
* miniature composition
* longer lenses when appropriate
* controlled depth of field
* strong geometric composition

## B. Sims-Style Camera

Used for:

* streets
* exterior buildings
* character following
* NPC interactions

Characteristics:

* human-scale framing
* readable environment
* moderate lens lengths
* smooth movement
* cinematic adaptation of the familiar Sims-style perspective

## C. Human Camera

Used for:

* bedroom
* bathroom
* emotional scenes
* character close-ups
* intimate moments

Characteristics:

* eye-level perspective
* cinematic lenses
* shallow depth of field
* stronger character framing

The camera should be able to move between these modes without making the world feel like three separate environments.

# 21. Cinematic Shot System

The camera system should eventually support a real shot-based workflow.

A shot should conceptually contain:

```text
SHOT
├── duration
├── camera
│   ├── position
│   ├── rotation
│   ├── lens
│   ├── focus
│   ├── aperture
│   └── depth of field
│
├── subject
├── target
├── movement
├── easing
├── look-at behavior
├── environment state
├── character state
├── lighting state
├── VFX state
└── music mappings
```

The sequence should be constructed from shots rather than requiring the user to manually animate one enormous camera timeline.

1. silhouette
2. readable subject
3. depth

5. color hierarchy
6. lighting
7. environmental storytelling

Every visual element should contribute to:

* story

# 26. Depth and Miniature Illusion

The miniature effect should be achieved through a combination of:

* depth of field
* lens choice
* camera height
* atmospheric perspective
* simplified materials
* object scale
* controlled lighting
* dense environmental composition

Tilt-shift should be available as an artistic tool but should not be permanently applied.

The miniature effect should remain subtle enough that close character scenes can still feel cinematic.

---

# 27. Lighting Philosophy

Lighting is a primary storytelling mechanism.

## Normal World

Use:

* soft daylight
* restrained nighttime illumination
* subtle practical lighting
* controlled shadows
* atmospheric depth

## Musical World

Gradually introduce:

* colored emissive sources
* volumetric light
* glowing windows
* neon accents
* colored reflections
* rim lighting
* bloom
* atmospheric color
* localized illumination

The important principle is:

> **Glow should illuminate the world, not simply appear as a bright overlay.**

Emissive materials should interact with the environment wherever technically feasible.

---

# 28. VFX Philosophy

VFX should feel physically integrated with the world.

Preferred:

* particles interacting with lighting
* volumetric effects
* environmental illumination
* reflections
* shadows
* atmospheric depth
* motion
* procedural deformation

Avoid relying exclusively on:

* flat screen-space overlays
* giant bloom
* arbitrary particle explosions
* generic lens flares
* random post-processing

VFX should look like something that exists **inside the miniature world**.

# 30. Asset Library Strategy

The asset acquisition process should prioritize **breadth first, hero quality second**.

First establish enough assets to make a convincing city.

Then identify the objects receiving substantial camera attention.

Those objects become candidates for:

* custom modeling
* custom materials
* custom shaders
* custom animation
* procedural behavior

The project should not attempt to custom-build everything.

# 34. Performance Philosophy

The art style must remain compatible with real-time rendering.

Stylization is not an excuse for uncontrolled asset complexity.

Prefer:

* modular assets
* instancing
* LODs
* hierarchical culling
* occlusion culling
* efficient materials
* texture atlasing where useful
* GPU-friendly particle systems
* controlled shadow distances
* selective high-quality effects

Visual importance should determine rendering cost.

A distant background building does not need the same treatment as the protagonist's bedroom.

---

# 35. Visual Consistency Rules

All assets should eventually conform to:

* common scale
* common material response
* common lighting assumptions
* common roughness language
* common color philosophy
* common bevel philosophy
* common level of abstraction
* common environmental density

The goal is for an audience to believe that the entire world was created by one art team.

Even when individual components originated from different sources.

---

# 36. What We Are NOT Building

This project is not intended to become:

* a realistic city simulator
* a traditional game
* a generic Unity-style asset showcase
* a photorealistic cinematic
* a conventional EDM visualizer
* a cyberpunk city
* a random procedural world
* an asset-store compilation

It is:

> **A cinematic musical world constructed like a miniature city and animated by the music itself.**

---

# 37. Creative North Star

When evaluating any new asset, effect, animation or feature, ask:

> **Does this make the miniature city feel more alive, more musical, more cinematic, or more magical?**

If not, it probably does not belong.

When evaluating an effect, ask:

> **Could this exist because the music is changing the world?**

If yes, it is probably aligned with the concept.

When evaluating an asset, ask:

> **Would this look like it belongs in the same miniature city as our gray clay protagonist?**

If no, normalize, stylize, replace or reject it.

---

# 38. Final Visual Identity

The finished music video should feel like:

> A tiny gray SimCity-like world sitting on a table.

> A gray clay man wakes up inside it.

> Music begins.

> One light turns on.

> Then another.

> A person starts moving.

> Color begins spreading through the streets.

> Buildings begin glowing.

> The city begins responding.

> The miniature world slowly becomes alive.

> The inhabitants begin dancing.

> Architecture begins moving with the rhythm.

> Streets become rivers of light.

> The skyline transforms.

> The physical rules of the city begin breaking.

> And eventually the entire miniature metropolis becomes a living audiovisual instrument.

The ultimate visual arc is:

# **GRAY → COLOR → GLOW → MOTION → TRANSFORMATION → SURREALISM**

That progression is the central artistic identity of the project.

Everything else should support it.
