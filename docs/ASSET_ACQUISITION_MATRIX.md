# AV Gen Music Video — Asset Acquisition Matrix

**Status:** Initial Production Planning  
**Related Documents:** `MUSIC_VIDEO_ART_DIRECTION.md`  
**Purpose:** Establish the initial 3D asset library required to build the city, characters, interiors, vehicles, street life, and musical world.

---

# 1. Acquisition Philosophy

We are **not** going to build the entire city from scratch.

The initial strategy is:

FIND GOOD ASSETS
        ↓
BUILD A COHERENT KIT
        ↓
NORMALIZE THEM
        ↓
BUILD CITY
        ↓
IDENTIFY VISUAL WEAKNESSES
        ↓
CUSTOMIZE IMPORTANT ASSETS
        ↓
REPLACE HERO ASSETS
        ↓
BUILD DISTINCTIVE MUSICAL ASSETS

The goal of the first asset pass is **not perfection**.

The goal is to obtain enough high-quality material to answer:

> **Can this actually look like the miniature music-video world we've designed?**

We should therefore prioritize **cohesive asset families** over individually spectacular models.

---

# 2. Asset Quality Tiers

Every asset should receive one of four classifications.

## Tier A — Hero

Camera will get close.

Must be excellent.

Potentially custom-built or heavily modified.

Examples:

- protagonist
- protagonist's bedroom
- important props
- hero buildings
- major musical objects

## Tier B — Midground

Frequently visible.

Should be good quality and stylistically compatible.

Examples:

- street buildings
- NPCs
- vehicles
- storefronts
- street furniture

## Tier C — Background

Primarily establishes density.

Can be relatively simple.

Examples:

- distant buildings
- distant vehicles
- rooftop equipment
- background trees

## Tier D — Procedural / Generated

Doesn't need to exist as a unique asset.

Should ideally be generated from systems.

Examples:

- repeated windows
- streetlights
- particles
- crowds
- vegetation distribution
- building variations
- road markings
- musical pulses

# 4. Character Assets

## 4.1 Protagonist

### Priority: CRITICAL

This is one of the few assets I would strongly consider custom-building early.

We need:

- male
- gray clay-like material
- stylized proportions
- clean topology
- expressive face
- simple clothing
- humanoid rig
- high-quality deformation

The character should be intentionally generic.

The audience should identify him through:

- silhouette
- behavior
- animation
- story
- camera language

rather than elaborate character design.

### Requirements

- humanoid skeleton
- facial controls if feasible
- clean deformation
- interchangeable materials
- interchangeable clothing
- good hands
- good feet
- expressive silhouette
- compatible with procedural animation

# 7. Musical Animation System

Do **not** simply buy a collection of dance animations and call the musical animation system finished.

The sourced animations provide the base material.

AV Gen should eventually layer musical modulation over them.

For example:

BASE WALK
     +
BODY SWAY
     +
HEAD BOB
     +
STEP OFFSET
     +
BEAT ACCENT
     +
ENERGY MODULATION

This is what will make the characters belong to the music-video world.

# 18. Musical Asset Library

This category should **not** be primarily sourced.

This is where AV Gen's identity begins.

We should custom-build:

- particle systems
- glowing materials
- light trails
- ribbons
- rings
- energy fields
- musical glyphs
- reactive vegetation
- reactive architecture
- procedural deformation
- environmental light pulses
- surreal structures

The music-reactive visual vocabulary should belong uniquely to AV Gen.

---

# 19. Asset Source Strategy

When evaluating sources, prioritize:

### 1. Consistent stylized packs

Best.

### 2. Modular environment kits

Excellent.

### 3. Individual high-quality assets

Good for filling gaps.

### 4. Generic realistic assets

Use sparingly and only if they can be successfully stylized.

Avoid acquiring huge quantities of:

> "Technically impressive but visually incompatible" assets.

One cohesive 20-piece kit is worth more than 100 unrelated models.
