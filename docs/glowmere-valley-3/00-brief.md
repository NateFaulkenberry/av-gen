# Glowmere Valley 3 — Autonomous Production Music Video

## Mission

Create **`glowmere-valley-3`**, a new AV Gen project derived from the existing:

`glowmere-valley-2-multicam`

This is the first attempt to use the current AV Gen Director system, character animation system, world/entity effects, modulation system, rendering pipeline, and scene utilities together to produce a **genuinely shippable music-video demo scene**.

This should **not** be treated primarily as another Auto-Director technology test.

Treat this as a **production exercise**:

> Given the tools currently available in AV Gen, can the agent independently direct, build, evaluate, refine, and polish a complete animated music video that feels intentionally authored by a professional VFX/music-video artist?

The finished project should be something a user could load into AV Gen as a showcase/demo project and immediately understand that it demonstrates the breadth of the engine.

The goal is not maximum feature count.

The goal is **a cohesive, entertaining, polished audiovisual experience in which the music, cinematography, animation, effects, environment, lighting, modulation, and color treatment all feel like parts of the same piece.**

# 3. Analyze the Music Yourself

Do not rely exclusively on AV Gen's built-in song analyzer.

Use the available audio-analysis tools as one source of information, but also perform an **independent musical/production analysis** of the actual audio.

The purpose is not simply to obtain timestamps.

The purpose is to understand the track.

Analyze things such as:

### Rhythm

* kick pattern
* 4-on-the-floor characteristics
* snare/clap/percussion patterns
* rhythmic density
* syncopation
* recurring rhythmic motifs
* beat strength
* downbeats
* phrase boundaries

### Energy

Determine how energy changes throughout the track.

Look for:

* low-energy passages
* sustained energy
* ramps
* accelerations
* anticipation
* releases
* peaks
* secondary peaks
* climax
* ending behavior

### Frequency characteristics

Look for meaningful changes in:

* sub bass
* bass
* low-mid energy
* midrange density
* high-frequency percussion
* spectral brightness

### Musical events

Identify meaningful events such as:

* kicks
* snares
* cymbals
* impacts
* bass hits
* transitions
* risers
* drops
* fills
* sustained notes
* repeated motifs
* significant changes in instrumentation

### Phrase structure

Determine where musical ideas begin and end.

The final sequence should feel synchronized to **musical phrases**, not merely individual beats.

# 10. Avoid the Diorama Problem

One known weakness of Glowmere Valley 2 is that wide shots can make the environment feel more like a **diorama** than an immersive valley.

This must be explicitly evaluated during production.

Do not simply avoid wide shots.

Instead investigate why they feel like a diorama.

Possible factors include:

* camera height
* camera distance
* field of view
* depth cues
* foreground elements
* atmospheric perspective
* fog
* lighting
* scale relationships
* terrain composition
* object density
* horizon treatment
* water placement
* vegetation distribution
* shadowing
* camera orientation
* lack of foreground occlusion
* overly clean visibility

Experiment with these factors.

Determine what makes the environment feel large and immersive.

Then refine the shots accordingly.

* Does the camera movement have purpose?
* Is the camera movement appropriate for the music?
* Does the shot feel cinematic?

### Animation

* Are characters behaving naturally enough for the intended style?
* Do their movements fit the shot?
* Are there visible animation problems?

### Environment

* Does the world feel immersive?
* Are there distracting geometry problems?
* Does the environment support the composition?

### Lighting

* Does the lighting establish hierarchy?
* Is the subject readable?
* Does it support the mood?

### Effects

* Do effects support the shot?
* Are they too weak?
* Are they too strong?
* Are they visually distracting?

### Music synchronization

* Does the shot align with the musical moment?
* Does the visual intensity correspond appropriately to musical energy?

# 19. Iterative Production Loop

The production process should resemble:

```text
ANALYZE
    ↓
PLAN
    ↓
BUILD
    ↓
RENDER
    ↓
INSPECT
    ↓
IDENTIFY PROBLEMS
    ↓
REVISE
    ↓
RENDER AGAIN
    ↓
COMPARE
    ↓
REFINE
    ↓
FINAL QUALITY PASS
```

Do not stop after the first successful render.

The agent should actively seek flaws.

For example:

> "This wide shot technically works, but the environment reads as a miniature."

Then change:

* camera
* lens/FOV
* foreground
* fog
* lighting
* composition

and render again.

Or:

> "The mushroom stem visibly intersects the terrain."

Then investigate a correction.

Or:

> "The riser does not visually escalate enough."

Then revise:

* camera motion
* shot duration
* effects
* lighting
* modulation
* color
* transitions

and evaluate again.

# Final Instruction

Take ownership of the production.

Make the creative decisions yourself.

Research where necessary.

Inspect the existing implementation before assuming something is missing.

Use the current AV Gen systems aggressively but intelligently.

Create your own directives.

Analyze the music yourself.

Build the sequence.

Render it.

Look at what you created.

Critique it.

Fix it.

Render it again.

Repeat until the remaining problems are genuinely minor.

**Attempt to improve it.**
