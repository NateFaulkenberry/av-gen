# 2. Art direction

## 2.1 What Glowmere Valley 3 should feel like

**The valley's light is the protagonist.** It wakes layer by layer as the track does. It dims when
a visitor passes over. It is drained to almost nothing while the saucer takes one of the valley's
animals. On the drop it comes back rebuilt, brighter and wider than it began.

This concept comes from the music, not from a list of features.

- **"Rebuild" builds by adding layers.** A riff at bar 5, a body at 17, a mid synth at 33, a
  shimmer at 41. A world whose light arrives the same way is a direct visual rhyme.
- **The track spends its drama in one place.** It subtracts, submerges, builds and drops across
  bars 81–97. Its only earlier gesture is a two-bar pull-back at 26 s, a promise kept later.
- **The film follows the same shape.** It holds one set-piece, the abduction, carried across
  exactly those bars. The pull-back becomes a foreshadowing: a shape crossing the sky while the
  music holds its breath.

The characters serve the concept:

- **The elder** is the valley's heart. It has the only warm colour in the world, its gold gills,
  and they carry the kick.
- **The aliens** are the valley's residents and its witnesses. They wander, investigate and gather
  in the glow. When the beam lights they come to watch, which is their own behaviour and not a
  script.
- **The horse** that grazes by the elder is what the valley loses.
- **The saucer** is the visitor: slow, heavy, and gone as suddenly as it came.

## 2.2 The visual arc

| | Segment (music) | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | cold open, riff groove | **Nocturne.** A night valley already breathing on the first kick, seen from inside it. The elder's gold is the one warm thing. |
| 0:26 | first pull-back | **A shadow passes.** The music drops out and so does the light. Something crosses the sky. |
| 0:30 | groove 2, lift | **Waking.** The light returns a little brighter. The aliens wander and the camera starts to travel with them, then rises for the first time. |
| 1:14 | arrival | **The valley lights up.** The shimmer arrives and everything answers: spores, fireflies, caps. The first grand wide is a place, not a model. |
| 1:29 | melodic plateau, lead forward | **Communion.** Long takes among the lit heroes. The aliens gather, then look up as the lead comes forward and the aurora answers it. |
| 2:28 | suspension | **The visitor.** The sparkle is cut from the music and the valley. The saucer comes in slow and heavy over the rim, and the light drains as it comes. |
| 2:43 | submerged break | **Submerged.** Dark, heavy and blue. Only the saucer's lights. |
| 2:50 | riser | **The lift.** The beam lights. The horse rises and glows, light floods back up the column, and the cutting accelerates with the roll. |
| 2:58 | drop | **Rebuilt.** The horse vanishes on the crash and the valley's light returns wider and brighter than ever. The saucer climbs away over the north rim until it is a star. |
| 3:42 | tail | **Afterglow.** One held image on kick and clap alone, then black on the last hit. |

## 2.3 Colour progression

The colour runs from affinity to contrast and back, but not back to where it began (research
principle 22).

- **Affinity:** navy and teal, with the elder's gold as the single accent.
- **Dimming:** saturation and exposure fall together at the pull-back, the suspension and the break,
  where the grade goes cool and nearly monochrome blue.
- **Opening:** at the arrival, saturation opens a little and warms. At the drop it opens fully
  (saturation 1.28, warmest temperature, brightest exposure), with the gold against the teal as the
  widest contrast in the film.
- **Resolution:** the tail settles warmer than the opening. A shifted end state is the "rebuilt"
  of the title.

## 2.4 Camera philosophy

**The valley is large, and the camera proves it.** Every rule below answers the diorama problem
([05-quality.md](05-quality.md) §diorama):

- **No depth of field on wide shots,** and never a vertical blur gradient (research principle 1).
  The source had physical depth of field focused at 8 m on every shot.
- **Eye height or low.** Cameras sit at an alien's eye height (about 3 m) or lower, or they are
  genuinely aerial with a horizon in frame. Never a 30–50° look-down from middle height, the
  tabletop view (principle 2).
- **Distance comes from long lenses far away,** not from wide lenses close up (principle 9).
- **Something close to the lens:** a fern, a stem or a cap edge on every wide, with lateral moves
  so the near layer parallaxes against the far one (principle 7).
- **Slow angular motion for big things.** No fast orbit of a small radius, which is the turntable
  (principle 4).
- **Cut on bars and phrases,** and on beats only in the riser and the first bars of the drop
  (principle 14).

## 2.5 Effects philosophy

Every effect needs a reason in the concept, or it goes.

- **Kept:**
  - the aurora, which carries the lead;
  - the spores and fireflies, which are the shimmer;
  - the tractor beam, which is the set-piece.
- **Removed:**
  - the camera travel beam, a violet streak across the sky every 9 s;
  - the sixteen hero ground-pulses, which fired on the old cut's schedule;
  - the saucer's kick-bob and beat-spin (a massive craft moves slowly).
- **Added**, each because a shot proved it needed it:
  - the horse's light: a gold Glow on the horse through the lift, because the source's glow
    multiplied an emission of zero and the horse was lost in the beam (F24);
  - one camera shake, on the crash (F33).

## 2.6 Modulation philosophy

The rule is a hierarchy, with one visual owner per musical layer, kept for the whole film
(research principles 12 and 13).

| Reacts | To | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| strongly, on the beat | the kick | the elder's gold only (its gills and the underside around them): the heartbeat |
| moderately | the two-bar bass glide | every cap's glow swells about 10%: the breath |
| moderately | the top of the mix (the shimmer and 16ths) | spores and fireflies |
| once | the crash at 177.7 s | a flash of exposure and bloom, and the film's one camera shake |
| once each | the four kick gaps (bars 24, 40, 56, 72) | the glow dips for a beat: a held breath |
| slowly | section by section | exposure, saturation, fog, the valley's light, the aurora (the arc) |
| barely | the bass | the river's surface roughness |
| never | anything | terrain, trees, the river's flow, the horizon, the camera's framing |

The clocks are the film's own score, not the engine's beat clock:
- the heartbeat is a timeline of the 475 kicks the track actually plays;
- the crash and the gaps are timelines of their own instants;
- the breath is an LFO locked to the measured grid.

The engine's bars start on beat 4 of this track, because its first tracked beat is counted as beat
1. Accents lead the beat by 20 ms (principle 17).
